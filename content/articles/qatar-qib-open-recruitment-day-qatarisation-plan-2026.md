Qatar's **Ministry of Labour** is stepping up efforts to get more citizens into private sector jobs. It recently held an **Open Recruitment Day** with **Qatar Islamic Bank (QIB)**, announced on **13 October 2026**, and has begun talks with state-owned companies on its **Private Sector Nationalisation Plan**.

## The QIB recruitment day

The event focused on **banking careers** and drew a large number of **Qatari graduates and job seekers**. The ministry said it supports:

| Group | Support |
|---|---|
| Qatari university students | Career guidance and links to employers |
| Recent Qatari graduates | Matching to private sector openings |
| Children of Qatari women | Eligible for the ministry's employment support |

The organisers did not publish the number of vacancies or the job titles. Banking is one of the main sectors where Qatarisation targets apply.

## Consultations on the nationalisation plan

In late September, Minister of Labour **Dr Ali bin Samikh Al Marri** chaired the first of a series of consultation sessions with chief executives of **state-owned companies** and public-benefit entities. The plan sets out **targets**, **incentives**, **partnerships** and **monitoring** to raise Qatari employment in companies, under the **Private Sector Jobs Nationalisation Law**.

## How to apply

Qatari job seekers should register on **Kawader**, the ministry's national employment platform, and follow the [Ministry of Labour](https://www.mol.gov.qa/) for upcoming recruitment days. QIB lists its own vacancies on its [official website](https://www.qib.com.qa/).

## What it means for job seekers

- **Qatari graduates:** Banking and finance employers are actively recruiting nationals. Attend open days with an updated CV.
- **Register on Kawader.** It is the main route the ministry uses to match citizens with company jobs.
- **State-owned firms will set targets.** Expect more Qatari intake programmes as the nationalisation plan moves ahead.
- **Expats:** Customer-facing and junior banking roles are increasingly being filled by Qatari staff, so specialist skills matter more.
