A small USCIS policy change that starts **today, 23 October 2026**, could decide whether an H-1B extension, an OPT application or a response to USCIS arrives "on time". Policy Alert **PA-2026-15**, titled *Definition of Day for Purposes of Filing Deadlines*, was issued on **23 September 2026** and applies to paper filings USCIS receives from 23 October onward.

The short version: the **next-business-day grace** for weekends and federal holidays now covers far fewer situations than before.

## Old rule vs new rule

| Situation | Under the 2023 guidance | From 23 October 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Paper filing, deadline counted in days, last day is a weekend or holiday | Next business day allowed | Still allowed, if USCIS **receives** it before the following business day closes |
| Deadline tied to a date or eligibility (I-94 expiry, age 21, status end date) | Weekend rule could apply | **No extra day** |
| Online filing via myUSCIS | — | Received when submitted, **no extra day** |
| Three extra days when a decision is served by mail | Applied | Unchanged |

Analysts also note that the online system generally runs on **UTC time**, so a midnight UTC cut-off falls on the previous evening in US time zones.

## Who should pay attention

- **H-1B workers and employers.** When an I-94 runs out on a Saturday or Sunday, the extension petition needs to be filed before that date, not on the Monday after.
- **F-1 students applying for OPT or STEM OPT.** Plan around the actual last date, especially if you file online.
- **Anyone answering a Request for Evidence or filing a motion or appeal.** Day-count windows can still roll over for paper filings, but USCIS counts the date it receives the package, not the date you post it.
- **Families with a child close to 21.** If the 21st birthday falls on a weekend, the filing must arrive before it.

The alert does **not** change eligibility rules for H-1B, F-1, OPT or any other category. It only changes how USCIS reads a deadline.

## What it means for job seekers on US visas

A missed date can mean a gap in status, a lost job start or a denied application. With the grace period narrowed, the safest plan is to treat every deadline as fixed and file early. Employers handling many visa workers may need to update their internal calendars and remind HR teams that the old "Monday is fine" habit no longer works for date-based cut-offs.

## Tips to avoid a late filing

1. **Sort your deadlines** into two lists: those counted in days and those tied to a specific date or age.
2. **Aim to file a week early**, whether on paper or online.
3. **Use tracked courier delivery** for paper filings and keep proof of the receipt date.
4. **Check the time zone** shown on your online account before a late-night submission.
5. **Send RFEs or denials to your lawyer straight away**, since response windows are short.
