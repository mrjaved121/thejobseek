Kuwait is weighing a rule that would stop any **single nationality** from making up more than **30% to 40%** of a company's workforce. First Deputy Prime Minister and Interior Minister **Sheikh Fahad Al-Yousef** described the idea at a meeting with business owners on **22 October 2026**, saying it is **under review**.

Under the plan as described, companies that go over the limit would pay **extra fees** rather than face an outright ban.

## Measures under study

| Proposal | What it would mean | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Nationality cap of 30–40% per company | Firms would need a more mixed workforce, or pay more | Under review |
| Domestic workers to private sector (Article 20 to Article 18) | Housemaids, drivers and others could switch to company jobs | Under study |
| Workers from finished government projects to private residency | Staff whose project ended could stay and work for other firms | Under study |
| Workers must work only at their assigned site | Firms breaking this risk having their file closed | Being enforced |

## Why Kuwait is looking at this

Kuwait has long tried to change the make-up of its population and labour market, where expatriates far outnumber citizens. The minister linked the wider crackdown to firms that hire workers without paying them, or that register people who never actually work for them. On the same day, officials said about [10,000 company files had been suspended over unpaid wages](/kuwait/kuwait-10000-companies-suspended-unpaid-wages-pam-wps-2026/).

## Who could be affected

- **Large nationality groups** in Kuwait's private sector could find some employers less able to hire more people from their country.
- **Workers from smaller communities** might see new openings as firms try to balance their staff.
- **Domestic workers** could gain a legal route into company jobs if the transfer plan is approved.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Nothing changes yet.** Do not trust agents who claim the cap is already law.
- **Follow official channels.** Watch announcements from PAM and the Ministry of Interior.
- **Domestic workers should keep documents in order.** A valid residency and clean record will matter if transfers open up.
- **Build skills that are hard to replace.** Specialist workers are less affected by quota rules.
