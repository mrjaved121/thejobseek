**If your private-sector job in Saudi Arabia ends, your employer owes you an end-of-service award: half a month's last wage for each of your first five years and a full month's wage for each year after that.** Resigning cuts the amount unless you have ten years or more. Your employer must pay within one or two weeks, and you should claim unpaid dues within 12 months. Below is the full calculation, with worked examples and the steps to claim.

We rebuilt this guide on 27 November 2026 from the English text of the [Saudi Labor Law published by HRSD](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-02/Labor.pdf) and the ministry's [Labor Culture page](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/labor-culture). For the 2025 amendments we used reports by [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2024/10/30/saudi-arabia-labour-law-what/), [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/649521/SAUDI-ARABIA/6-week-mandatory-maternity-leave-while-probationary-period-is-180-days) and the law firm [Al Tamimi & Company](https://www.tamimi.com/news/key-amendments-to-saudi-arabias-labour-law/), all opened the same day.

## Who qualifies for the end-of-service award?

The award covers workers under the Saudi Labor Law, which means private-sector employees, Saudi or expatriate. According to Article 84, it is due "upon the end of the employment relation", whatever the reason, with three main exceptions:

- **You resigned with less than two years of service.** Article 85 gives no award in that case.
- **You were dismissed under Article 80.** That article lists serious cases, such as assaulting a manager, forging documents to get the job, disclosing trade secrets, or unexcused absence of over 30 days in one contract year or over 15 days in a row (a written warning must come first). In those cases the employer may end the contract without an award, notice or compensation, but must first let you state your objection.
- **Your contract excludes some pay items.** Article 86 lets the parties agree that commissions, sales percentages and similar variable pay are left out of the wage used for the award.

The HRSD page explains that "wage" in the law means the **actual wage**: your basic pay plus regular allowances and increments tied to your work. So the figure to use is your last full monthly wage, unless your contract lawfully excludes variable items.

## How is the award calculated?

The formula in Article 84 has two bands, and part-years are paid in proportion.

| Service | Award for each year | Example at SAR 6,000 a month |
|---|---|---|
| Years 1 to 5 | Half a month's last wage | SAR 3,000 a year |
| Year 6 onwards | One month's last wage | SAR 6,000 a year |
| Part of a year | Pro rata | 6 months in year 3 = SAR 1,500 |

**Example 1: contract ended by the employer.** Last wage SAR 6,000, seven years of service. The first five years give 5 × 3,000 = SAR 15,000. Years six and seven give 2 × 6,000 = SAR 12,000. The total is **SAR 27,000**.

**Example 2: part-year.** Last wage SAR 5,000, three years and six months. That is 3.5 × 2,500 = **SAR 8,750**.

## How much do I get if I resign?

Article 85 reduces the award when the job ends because you resigned. First work out the full amount as above, then apply the share for your length of service.

| Service when you resign | Share you receive | Example: SAR 6,000 wage |
|---|---|---|
| Under 2 years | Nothing | SAR 0 |
| 2 to 5 years | One third | 3 years: 9,000 ÷ 3 = SAR 3,000 |
| Over 5 and under 10 years | Two thirds | 7 years: 27,000 × 2/3 = SAR 18,000 |
| 10 years or more | Full award | 10 years: SAR 45,000 |

There are exceptions. Under Article 87 you keep the **full award** if you leave because of force majeure beyond your control. A female worker who quits within six months of her wedding, or within three months of childbirth, is also paid in full. Under Article 81 you may leave **without notice** and keep your statutory rights if, for example, the employer fails its essential obligations, misled you about the job when you signed, or treats you with cruelty or insult.

Not renewing a fixed-term contract is different from resigning. The HRSD Labor Culture page says that telling your employer you do not want to renew is a right of both parties and is not treated as a resignation, as long as you give any notice the contract requires.

## What else is in the final settlement?

The end-of-service award is only one part. The Labor Law adds:

- **Unused annual leave.** Article 109 gives at least 21 days of paid leave a year, rising to at least 30 days after five consecutive years with the same employer. Article 111 says you must be paid for leave days you did not use, plus leave pay for the part of the year you worked.
- **Compensation for an invalid termination.** If the employer ends the contract without a valid reason, Article 77 gives 15 days' wage per year of service on an indefinite contract, or the wage for the rest of the term on a fixed-term contract, with a minimum of two months' wage. HRSD notes that this comes on top of the end-of-service award and leave pay.
- **Return ticket and fees.** Article 40 makes the employer pay recruitment fees, iqama and work permit fees, exit and re-entry visas and your ticket home when the job ends. You pay your own ticket only if you are unfit for work or leave without a legitimate reason.
- **Experience certificate.** Article 64 says the employer must give you a free certificate on request, showing your start and end dates, job and last salary.

## What changed in the 2025 amendments?

The National reported that amendments to the Labor Law took effect on 18 February 2025, and Saudi Gazette reported the same changes as they came into force. The end-of-service formula above did not change, but these points affect how a job ends:

- **Probation:** up to 180 days, and either side may end the contract during it.
- **Notice on indefinite contracts:** 30 days from the worker and 60 days from the employer, according to Saudi Gazette and The National. Al Tamimi's summary says the 30-day rule for workers is written for Saudi nationals, so check your own contract.
- **Contract term for expats:** Al Tamimi says an indefinite contract for a non-Saudi worker is now treated as a one-year fixed term that renews each year.
- **Resignation:** deemed accepted if the employer does not answer within 30 days. The employer may delay acceptance by up to 60 days with written reasons, and you can withdraw within seven days if it has not been accepted.
- **Leave:** 12 weeks of paid maternity leave and three days off when a brother or sister dies.

## How do I claim my end-of-service award? Step by step

1. **Check your contract on Qiwa.** HRSD says contracts are authenticated on the [Qiwa platform](https://www.qiwa.sa/en) to protect both sides. Note your start date, last wage and contract type.
2. **Work out your figure.** Use the tables above. Count exact months, not just full years.
3. **Ask for a written settlement.** Request a breakdown of the award, leave pay and any compensation. Also ask for your experience certificate under Article 64.
4. **Wait the legal period.** Article 88 gives the employer one week to pay when it ended the contract, or two weeks when you ended it. The employer may deduct work-related debts you owe it.
5. **Keep your records.** Save your payslips, bank statements showing salary deposits, your resignation or termination letter and all messages.
6. **Complain to HRSD if you are not paid.** The ministry's website has "Make a Complaint" and "Digital Complaint" links on every page, including the [Labor Culture page](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/labor-culture).
7. **Go to the labour court before the deadline.** Article 234 says a labour court may not hear a claim filed more than **12 months** after the job ended, unless it accepts your excuse or the employer admits the debt. Do not wait until you are home.

## Tips for applicants from Pakistan and India

These points matter when you sign a Saudi job, because they shape what you can claim when it ends.

**Contract and attestation.** Pakistan's [Overseas Employment Corporation](https://oec.gov.pk/protector-process-guide) lists an original, attested job contract among the papers needed for the Protector of Emigrants stamp. Read the wage figure on it closely: your end-of-service award is calculated on the last wage, so an inflated "package" with a small basic pay can hurt you later.

**Medicals.** Workers heading to the Gulf book the GCC medical through [Wafid](https://wafid.com/), run by the Gulf Health Council, which lists approved centres and lets you view results. Book only through Wafid's own site.

**Police clearance.** The Labor Law and HRSD pages we checked do not set a police certificate rule. Get one only if your employer or the Saudi visa process asks, and only through your own government's official service.

**Pakistan: BE&OE and the Protector of Emigrants.** The OEC guide says the protector stamp is compulsory for every Pakistani going abroad for work, including the Gulf. You visit a Protectorate office, pay fees by bank challan (only through Bank of Punjab or National Bank, it says), give biometrics and collect your stamped passport, usually in three to five working days. Protectorates are part of the Bureau of Emigration and Overseas Employment, whose site is [beoe.gov.pk](https://beoe.gov.pk/). It showed us an access error on 27 November 2026, so ask the Protectorate office itself for today's fee list.

**India: eMigrate, ECR and ECNR.** India's official [eMigrate site](https://emigrate.gov.in/) names Saudi Arabia among 18 ECR countries. If your passport is ECR, you need emigration clearance before you leave, and a Pravasi Bharatiya Bima Yojana (PBBY) policy covering INR 10 lakh (the premium is INR 275 for a two-year policy, INR 375 for three years). ECNR holders can register voluntarily. If your dues are unpaid, the portal's Pravasi Bhartiya Sahayata Kendra helpline is 1800 11 3090 (WhatsApp +91 74283 21144).

**Scam warning.** Article 40 puts recruitment fees on the employer. Never pay an agent for a Saudi job, visa or interview, and never sign away your end-of-service rights to get a release. Pakistani readers can [check that a promoter is licensed](/pakistan/verify-licensed-overseas-employment-promoter-pakistan/) before dealing with one.

## Quick checklist before you leave a Saudi job

- Last wage and start date confirmed on Qiwa
- Award worked out with the two-band formula and, if you resigned, the right share
- Unused leave days counted
- Settlement and experience certificate requested in writing
- Complaint filed with HRSD if unpaid after one or two weeks
- Court claim, if needed, within 12 months
