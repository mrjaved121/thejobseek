The UK job market cooled again this autumn. Figures released by the **Office for National Statistics (ONS)** on **20 October 2026** show fewer open jobs than in the previous quarter and the previous year.

## The numbers at a glance

| Measure | Latest figure | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Job vacancies (Jul–Sep 2026) | **691,000** | down 21,000 (2.9%) on the quarter; down 37,000 (5.1%) on the year |
| Unemployment rate (Jun–Aug 2026) | **4.7%** | down 0.1 points on the quarter and the year |
| Employment rate, ages 16–64 | **75.3%** | up 0.2 points |
| Regular pay growth (excluding bonuses) | **3.7%** a year | broadly stable |
| Payrolled employees (Sep 2026, early estimate) | **30.3 million** | up 13,000 on August |

Over the year to August 2026, the number of employees on payrolls fell by 111,000.

## What this means if you want to work in the UK

- **Fewer vacancies means more competition.** With fewer openings than a year ago, employers can be more selective. A strong, UK-style CV and relevant experience matter more.
- **Sponsorship is still essential.** Most overseas workers need an employer with a sponsor licence and a role that qualifies for a Skilled Worker visa. Check that an employer is a licensed sponsor before applying.
- **Wages are still rising,** with regular pay up 3.7% a year, but salary thresholds for visas apply, so confirm the role's pay meets the requirement.
- **Recent rule changes:** see our summary of the [UK immigration rule changes from 8 October 2026](/uk/uk-immigration-rules-changes-8-october-2026/).

The next ONS labour market update is due on **17 November 2026**.
