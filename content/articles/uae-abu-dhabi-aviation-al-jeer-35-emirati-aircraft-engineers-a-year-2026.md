Young Emiratis interested in **airline jobs in the UAE** can earn a salary while they train. **Abu Dhabi Aviation Group** says its **Al Jeer** aircraft engineering programme will enrol **35 new Emirati students every year**, and each one is **employed by the group from day one of their studies**. The programme recently marked its first anniversary, Gulf News reported.

## Programme at a glance

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Programme | Al Jeer aircraft engineering scheme |
| Employer | Abu Dhabi Aviation Group |
| Intake | 35 Emirati students a year |
| Currently enrolled | More than 65 UAE nationals |
| Employment | Paid employee from the first day of study |
| Licences | GCAA B1, B1.3 or B2 aircraft maintenance licences |
| Practical training | Two years of supervised work |
| Partner | Mawaheb Talent Hub (Department of Government Enablement) |

## How the training works

Trainees do not stay in one workshop. They rotate across the group's companies, including **Etihad Engineering**, **Global Aerospace Logistics (GAL)**, **AMMROC** and **Abu Dhabi Aerospace Services**. This gives them hands-on experience on commercial and military aircraft, engines and components.

The two years of supervised practice count towards a **GCAA aircraft maintenance licence** in category B1 (mechanical), B1.3 (helicopters) or B2 (avionics). These licences are recognised qualifications that lead to well-paid, long-term careers in aviation.

## Who can apply

Al Jeer is aimed at **UAE nationals**. Selection is competitive: about one in three candidates who reach the final interview is accepted. Group CEO Mahmood Alhay Al Hamli said the scheme lets young Emiratis begin their careers while they are still studying.

## How to apply

Abu Dhabi Aviation Group has not published a separate online form for Al Jeer. Emiratis interested in the next intake should watch the group's official channels and the Mawaheb Talent Hub, which runs the programme with the group, for announcements.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Strong maths and physics grades** help, as aircraft engineering is technical.
- **Get ready for interviews** – only a third of finalists are selected, so practise explaining why you want an aviation career.
- **Think long term** – licensed engineers are in short supply worldwide, and the UAE's airlines and MRO firms keep expanding.
- **Expats** cannot join Al Jeer, but MRO companies in Abu Dhabi also hire licensed engineers from overseas.
