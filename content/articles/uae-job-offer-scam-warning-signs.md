Thousands of people from Pakistan, India and other countries search for jobs in Dubai every day, and scammers know it. Fake job offers usually look professional, use the names of real companies, and arrive through WhatsApp, Facebook or email. The goal is almost always the same: to take money from you before you realise the job does not exist.

Here are the warning signs to look for before you trust any UAE job offer.

## 1. They ask you to pay for the job or the visa

In the UAE, the employer is responsible for the cost of your work permit and residence visa. If someone asks you to pay a "visa fee", "processing fee", "medical fee" or "security deposit" to secure the job, treat it as a red flag.

## 2. You got the offer without a real interview

Real employers interview candidates, by phone, video or in person. An offer that arrives after a short WhatsApp chat, or with no interview at all, is a strong warning sign.

## 3. The email address is not a company address

Large companies send offers from their own domain, such as name@company.ae or name@company.com. Offers from Gmail, Yahoo or Hotmail addresses using a big company's name should be checked very carefully.

## 4. The salary is too good to be true

A very high salary for a basic role with no experience required, plus free accommodation, flights and car, is a classic trick to make you stop asking questions.

## 5. They want you to come on a visit visa and "convert" later

A visit visa does not allow you to work in the UAE. Working without a proper work permit can lead to fines, deportation and a ban. A genuine employer arranges the correct work permit.

## 6. Pressure to decide fast

"Only 2 seats left", "pay today or lose the job" — urgency is used to stop you from checking the offer with family or official sources.

## 7. Poor spelling and vague details

Real offer letters clearly state the job title, salary breakdown, working hours, contract type and company address. Vague documents full of mistakes are suspicious.

## 8. The company cannot be found

Search the company's official website and phone number yourself — not the ones the recruiter sent you — and contact the company directly to confirm the offer.

## How to check an offer safely

- **Search the company yourself** and call its official number to confirm the vacancy and the recruiter's name.
- **Never send money** for a job or visa to an individual's account or through money transfer apps.
- **In Pakistan,** use only overseas employment promoters licensed by the Bureau of Emigration & Overseas Employment, and check the licence on its official website.
- **In the UAE,** private sector work permits are issued through the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). Ask the employer how your permit will be processed.

> If you have already paid a fake recruiter, keep all chats, receipts and account details and report it to the police and the platform where you were contacted.

A real job never requires you to buy it. If anything about an offer feels wrong, stop and verify before you pay or travel.
