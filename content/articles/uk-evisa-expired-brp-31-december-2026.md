The UK has moved to **eVisas**, a digital record of your immigration status. The Home Office says **all biometric residence permits (BRPs) have now expired** and been replaced. If you still hold an expired BRP, you have until **31 December 2026** at the latest to use it to get into your online account.

Some social media posts say every visa holder "must use eVisas by 31 December 2026". That is not quite right. Here is the actual rule.

## The rule at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| BRP status | All expired, replaced by eVisas |
| What an expired BRP can still do | Create or sign in to a UKVI account, view eVisa, get a share code, apply to extend |
| Leniency ends | 31 December 2026, or 24 months after the BRP's expiry date if sooner |
| Right to work and right to rent checks | Must use eVisa share code |
| Visa stickers (vignettes) in passports | Valid until they expire |
| Settlement on old paper documents | eVisa optional |

## What to do now

1. **Create your UKVI account** if you have not done so, using your expired BRP while you still can.
2. **Check that your eVisa details are correct**, including your current passport.
3. **Update your passport details** in your UKVI account whenever you renew your passport, so you can travel without problems.
4. **Use share codes** for job applications and renting.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Employers will ask for a share code**, not a BRP card. Generate one from your UKVI account before interviews.
- **Fix problems early.** If your eVisa is missing or wrong, contact UKVI before you change jobs or travel.
- **Be wary of "eVisa agents".** Creating a UKVI account and getting an eVisa is free on GOV.UK.
