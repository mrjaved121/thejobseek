If you are hunting for **UK graduate jobs** in engineering, the aerospace sector just got a major boost. Announced alongside the Budget on **28 October 2026**, the UK government and **Rolls-Royce** have agreed a joint **£2.5 billion, 10-year programme** to design and build the next generation of jet engines in Britain. The government says the work could support around **3,300 existing jobs** in the next few years and open the door to many new ones over the long term.

## The deal at a glance

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Partners | UK Government and Rolls-Royce |
| Value | £2.5 billion, shared |
| Length | 10 years |
| Jobs supported | About 3,300 in the next few years |
| Longer term | Tens of thousands more possible |
| Main location | Rolls-Royce UK sites and suppliers, mostly outside London and the South East |
| Focus | Engines for single-aisle passenger jets and military aircraft |

## What the programme is about

The money is aimed at engines for **single-aisle aircraft**, the planes that make up the biggest part of the world's civil aviation market. Rolls-Royce estimates that getting a British engine onto these jets could be worth up to **£120 billion** to the UK economy over the life of the programme.

The work will cover a wide range of technology, including **advanced materials, electronic systems, advanced manufacturing, robotics and artificial intelligence**. The agreement is also designed to help UK suppliers win a share of the work, which spreads the jobs well beyond Rolls-Royce itself.

One point to note: **no grants have been paid out yet**. Future support still has to pass legal, affordability, value-for-money and subsidy control checks, and the final terms with Rolls-Royce need to be agreed.

## Which jobs could follow

A long engine programme like this needs people at every stage, from design offices to factory floors. Typical roles in aerospace engine work include:

- Mechanical, aerospace and materials engineers
- Software, controls and electronics engineers
- Manufacturing and production engineers
- Machinists, fitters and technicians
- Quality, test and inspection specialists
- Data, AI and robotics specialists
- Supply chain and project management staff

## How to apply

Rolls-Royce advertises its UK vacancies, graduate programmes and apprenticeships on its own official careers website. Search for "Rolls-Royce careers" and use the UK filters to see open roles. Supplier companies will also recruit as the programme grows, so watch the careers pages of aerospace firms near Rolls-Royce sites.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Students and graduates:** engineering, physics, maths and computer science degrees are a strong fit. Apply early for graduate schemes, as places fill quickly.
- **School leavers:** aerospace firms run apprenticeships in engineering and manufacturing that let you earn while you train.
- **Experienced engineers:** skills in materials, controls, robotics and digital manufacturing will be in demand.
- **Think regionally:** the government expects most jobs outside London and the South East, so look at the Midlands, the North and other industrial areas.
- **Be patient:** this is a 10-year plan, so hiring will build up over time rather than all at once.
