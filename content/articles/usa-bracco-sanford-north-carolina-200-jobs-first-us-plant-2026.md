Italian diagnostic imaging company **Bracco** is building its **first manufacturing plant in the United States**, and it will need **200 workers**. For people searching for **USA jobs for foreigners** with a global employer, as well as local job seekers, this is a project to watch. North Carolina Governor **Josh Stein** announced on **27 October 2026** that Bracco will invest **$230 million** in a new plant in **Sanford, Lee County**.

## What Bracco will make

The new site will be a **sterile manufacturing plant** producing **injectable contrast media**. These are the liquids injected into patients before some scans so that organs and blood vessels show up clearly on the images. Bracco says making them in the US will shorten delivery times, cut transport costs and bring supply closer to American hospitals.

Bracco is a family-owned company headquartered in **Milan, Italy**.

The state grant is paid only if Bracco meets its hiring and investment targets each year. No construction or hiring schedule has been published yet.

## Which jobs are coming

The governor's office lists these areas:

- **Manufacturing** – production operators and sterile processing staff
- **Engineering** – process, equipment and facilities engineers
- **Technical** – maintenance and lab technicians
- **Quality** – quality assurance and quality control roles
- **Warehousing** – materials handling and logistics
- **Administration** – office and support roles

Training partners include **Central Carolina Community College**, the **NC Community College System** and the **NC Biotechnology Center**.

## How to apply

Bracco has not opened applications for Sanford yet. Check the careers section of Bracco's official website and set an alert for North Carolina roles. Local training courses at Central Carolina Community College are also worth watching, as they often link directly to new employers.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Life sciences skills are in demand.** Experience in clean rooms, sterile production or GMP (good manufacturing practice) is a big plus.
- **Get a biotech certificate.** North Carolina community colleges run short courses that prepare people for pharma production jobs.
- **Work rights matter.** Foreign applicants will need US work authorisation; the announcement does not mention sponsorship.
- **Be patient.** A new sterile plant takes time to build, so many roles will open over the next few years.
