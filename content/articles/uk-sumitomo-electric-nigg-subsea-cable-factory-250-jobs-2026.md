The UK's first factory for **high-voltage subsea power cables** is now open in the Scottish Highlands. Japan's **Sumitomo Electric** opened the **£350 million** plant at the **Port of Nigg** on **19 October 2026**, and says it will create **up to 250 direct jobs**, with **more than 400** more supported across the UK supply chain.

Recruitment is already under way, with factory and technical roles listed on the company's careers site.

| Area | Inverness and Cromarty Firth Green Freeport |

## Why it matters

Until now, the UK could not make the **HVDC subsea cables** that carry electricity from offshore wind farms to the grid. The Nigg factory changes that. Its first cables are linked to big grid projects run by **SSEN Transmission** and **National Grid**.

This kind of plant needs a steady, skilled workforce for many years, as more offshore wind and grid links are built around the UK.

## Training on offer

Sumitomo has trained local workers in **Japan**: 13 Scottish staff have already learned quality and precision skills there. **UHI Inverness** has run a course on undersea cable manufacturing for local employees, and the **Green Freeport** offers apprenticeships and training pathways.

## How to apply

Apply through Sumitomo Electric UK Power Cables' official careers page: [jobs.jobvite.com/sumitomo-electric-power-cables/jobs](https://jobs.jobvite.com/sumitomo-electric-power-cables/jobs). Engineers who do not see a matching role can join the **engineering register of interest**. No closing dates are shown, so apply early.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Highland workers have a new local employer.** Production and maintenance roles suit people from oil and gas, fabrication or other plant work.
- **Electrical skills are in demand.** Maintenance technicians keep cable lines running around the clock.
- **Training lowers the barrier.** Operator roles come with company training, so heavy industry experience can be enough.
- **More may follow.** The construction firm behind the plant said Sumitomo has further plans as the industry grows.
