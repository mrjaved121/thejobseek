Ontario wants to give **Canada hiring 2026** a lift. The provincial government has proposed a new **Ontario Competitiveness Tax Cut** that it says will support about **30,000 jobs**. Finance Minister **Peter Bethlenfalvy** announced the plan on **28 October 2026**, ahead of the province's Fall Economic Statement the next day.

The idea is simple: make it cheaper for companies to invest in Ontario, so they build, expand and hire there.

## Key numbers

| Measure | Government estimate |
|---|---|
| Tax relief | More than $8 billion over 5 years |
| With Budget 2026 measures | More than $12 billion |
| Jobs supported | About 30,000 |
| Extra business investment | At least $11 billion |
| Boost to GDP | About $6 billion |

## How the tax cut works

Businesses would be able to deduct the **full cost of qualifying investments right away**, instead of spreading it over many years. Qualifying items include:

- **Machinery and equipment**
- **Software**
- **Fibre-optic cable**
- **Patents**
- **Some vehicles and transport infrastructure**

The province says this would cut the tax rate on new business investment by 6.1 points, to about **3.6%**. That compares with an average of 16.9% in the US and 19% in the OECD.

The plan is designed to work alongside the federal **Productivity Mega Deduction** and needs federal legislation to pass. A separate small business tax cut from Budget 2026 already saves eligible firms up to $5,000 a year.

## A forecast, not job adverts

The 30,000 figure is a government projection. No sectors, employers or timelines have been named. Canadian HR Reporter also noted that some firms may spend the savings on automation rather than new staff. So there is nothing to apply for yet, but the plan shows where investment, and hiring, may grow.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Watch capital-heavy sectors.** Manufacturing, logistics, telecoms and tech are the most likely to expand.
- **Automation skills are useful.** If firms buy new machines and software, they will need people to install, run and maintain them.
- **Trades remain in demand.** Electricians, millwrights and machinists support new equipment.
- **Search official listings.** Use the Government of Canada's Job Bank and employers' own careers pages for Ontario openings.
