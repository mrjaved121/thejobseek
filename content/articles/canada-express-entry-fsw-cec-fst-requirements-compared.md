**Express Entry** is Canada's online system for skilled workers who want permanent residence. It covers three programs, and you must qualify for at least one of them to enter the pool. Here is how they compare, based on IRCC's official comparison.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Requirement | Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) | Canadian Experience Class (CEC) | Federal Skilled Trades (FST) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Language** | CLB **7** | CLB **7** for TEER 0/1; CLB **5** for TEER 2/3 | CLB **5** speaking and listening; CLB **4** reading and writing |
| **Work experience** | **1 year** continuous in the last **10 years** | **1 year in Canada** in the last **3 years** | **2 years** in a trade in the last **5 years** |
| **Job offer** | Not required, but adds points | Not required | **Required**, or a Canadian certificate of qualification |
| **Education** | Secondary education required; more earns points | Not required | Not required |
| **TEER levels** | 0, 1, 2, 3 | 0, 1, 2, 3 | Certain trades in TEER 2 and 3 |

Part-time work can count towards experience in all three, as long as the total adds up to the required amount.

## Which program fits you?

**Living in the Gulf with no Canadian experience?** FSW is usually the route. You need strong English (CLB 7 or higher) and at least one year of skilled work in a TEER 0 to 3 job.

**Already working in Canada on a work permit?** CEC is often the fastest path, because it needs no job offer and no education assessment.

**A skilled tradesperson?** FST covers trades such as industrial, electrical and construction trades, and chefs, but you need a job offer or a certificate of qualification from a Canadian authority.

## Language scores matter most

Your language test is often the biggest factor in your score. Only approved tests count: CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training and PTE Core for English, and TEF Canada and TCF Canada for French. Read: [Which English tests count for Express Entry](/canada/canada-express-entry-language-tests-ielts-celpip-pte-core/).

## Find your TEER level first

Before you check eligibility, find your **NOC code** and **TEER** level. Read: [Canada NOC codes and TEER categories explained](/canada/canada-noc-teer-categories-explained/).

## Tips

- **Meet the minimum, then aim higher.** Higher language scores earn more points.
- **Get reference letters** that list your duties, hours, salary and dates.
- **Watch category-based draws.** Canada also invites candidates in certain occupations. See: [Express Entry changes for November 2026](/canada/canada-express-entry-changes-november-2026-high-wage-occupations/).

> Requirements can change. Always confirm on IRCC's official website before you apply.
