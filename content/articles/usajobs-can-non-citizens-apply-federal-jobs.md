**USAJOBS** is the official website for US federal government jobs. If you are not a US citizen, here is the honest picture: **most federal jobs are not open to you**, but there are some exceptions.

## The general rule

You normally must be a **US citizen or national** to work for the federal government. Every job advert has a **"This job is open to"** section that says whether non-citizens can apply. Read it first.

## Competitive service

Most federal jobs are in the **competitive service**. Under **Executive Order 11935**, only citizens and nationals can compete for these jobs.

An agency can hire a non-citizen only if:

- **no qualified citizen** is available, **and**
- the **Office of Personnel Management (OPM)** approves.

Even then, the person gets an excepted appointment without competitive status, which limits later promotion or transfer.

## Excepted service and SES

Some agencies, such as the **US Postal Service**, the **Tennessee Valley Authority** and the **FBI**, and some roles, such as lawyers and chaplains, are outside the competitive rules. A non-citizen can be hired here only if **three things allow it**: the annual appropriations law, immigration law, and the agency's own policies.

## The appropriations ban and its exceptions

Each year, Congress bars agencies from using appropriated funds to pay non-citizens working **inside the US**. Exceptions include:

- **Lawful permanent residents** (green card holders) who are seeking citizenship
- **Refugees and asylees** who have declared their intention to become permanent residents and then citizens
- Temporary translators
- Emergency field workers for up to 60 days
- Some non-resident **wildland firefighters** for up to 120 days, under agreements with other countries

Some agencies are exempt from this ban.

## You still need the right to work

Whatever the job, you must be legally allowed to work in the US, as a citizen, a permanent resident, or someone authorised by US immigration authorities.

## What it means for you

- **On a work visa?** Federal jobs are very hard to get. Private sector jobs, universities and some state or local governments are more realistic.
- **Green card holder?** You may qualify for some posts, but check the "open to" section of each advert.
- **Abroad?** Non-citizens can sometimes work at US embassies and missions as locally employed staff, which follows different rules.
