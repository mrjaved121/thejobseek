Teenagers and young adults looking for **New Jersey youth jobs** have a bigger chance next summer. The state is putting **$9 million** into its paid **Summer Youth Work Experience Program (SYWEP)** and has opened it to residents aged **14 to 24**, the New Jersey Department of Labor and Workforce Development said on 5 November 2026.

## What does SYWEP offer?

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Funding | $9 million (up $1 million) |
| Eligible ages | 14 to 24 (previously from 16) |
| Paid work | 160 to 200 hours with local employers |
| Extras | Career-readiness training and support services |
| Priority | Young people on SNAP or TANF benefits |
| Track record | 11,779 young people and 500+ worksites since 2019 |

## Who can apply?

New Jersey residents aged 14 to 24 can take part, whether they are students or **not enrolled in school**. This year the state is also reaching out to young people in communities affected by violence and those involved with the justice system.

## What did officials say?

Governor **Mikie Sherrill** described a paid summer job as a way to give young people new opportunities and strengthen communities. Acting Labor Commissioner **Kevin D. Jarvis** said a summer job can change what a young person believes is possible.

## How does the programme work?

The state funds **local workforce development boards** and **community groups**, which then recruit participants and match them with employers. New Jersey started SYWEP as a pilot in 2019 to tackle poverty and create routes into work, and has invested more than **$39 million** so far.

## How do I sign up?

Contact your county workforce development board or a local community organisation taking part, and check updates from the **[New Jersey Department of Labor](https://www.nj.gov/labor/)**. Employers and groups can also offer to host worksites.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Younger teens can now earn**, from age 14.
- **Paid experience builds a CV** before college or full-time work.
- **Sign up early**, as local places are limited.
