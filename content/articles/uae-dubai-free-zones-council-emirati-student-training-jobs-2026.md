**Emirati university students will get practical training at leading companies in Dubai's free zones under a new initiative approved by the Dubai Free Zones Council.** The programme will link training to priority sectors and future skills, and it is designed so that companies can recruit the trainees they want, creating a route from a placement to a full-time job. The decision was taken at the council's 36th meeting on 9 November 2026, chaired by **Sheikh Ahmed bin Saeed Al Maktoum**.

## What is the new free zone training scheme?

Dubai has more than 20 free zones, home to thousands of international firms in sectors such as finance, technology, logistics, media and healthcare. Until now, Emirati students have had to find placements with these companies largely on their own.

The new initiative changes that. The council will work with universities to:

- match training placements with **priority sectors** and **future-focused specialisations**
- give students hands-on experience and access to company expertise
- improve students' readiness for the job market
- help companies identify and hire the Emirati talent they need

The council said the plan supports Emiratisation goals, builds a future-ready workforce and adds to the competitiveness of the national economy.

## What else did the council discuss?

Several other items affect people working in free zones:

- **Digital employment contract:** the council said work is complete on a digital contract covering work permit issuance and the cancellation or amendment of employment contracts.
- **Residence permit renewals:** a service to renew residence permits for free zone employees is being activated.
- **Worker protection:** a unified framework to protect workers' rights and make sure dues are paid on time is being developed.
- **Dubai Future Week:** the event runs from **18 to 21 November**, with around 75 events and an expected 10,000 participants.

## Which sectors could offer placements?

The council has not named the companies taking part. Based on the type of firms in Dubai free zones, likely areas include:

- technology, AI and software
- banking, fintech and professional services
- logistics, aviation and trade
- media and creative industries
- healthcare and life sciences

These are examples only, not confirmed partners.

## How can Emirati students take part?

No application page has been published yet. Students should ask their university careers office about the programme, follow the free zone authorities (such as DMCC, Dubai Silicon Oasis and Dubai Airport Freezone) for announcements, and keep their profiles up to date on the **Nafis** platform.

## What it means for job seekers

- Free zone firms, often seen as hard to enter for fresh graduates, will now have a formal link with Emirati students.
- Choosing a degree in a priority sector could improve your chances of a placement.
- A good training placement can become a job offer, so treat it like a long interview.
- Dubai Future Week (18–21 November) is a chance to meet employers in person.

Other Dubai entities run similar schemes; see [Dubai Municipality's Future Talents programme for Emirati students](/uae/uae-dubai-municipality-future-talents-2026-emirati-students-jobs/).
