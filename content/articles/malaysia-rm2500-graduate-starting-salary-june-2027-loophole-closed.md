Fresh graduates looking at **Malaysia jobs** are set for a pay floor. Malaysia plans a **RM2,500 monthly starting salary** for graduates and semi-skilled workers from **June 2027**, and the government says employers will **not be able to avoid it** by quietly lowering the qualifications a job asks for.

## What did the government say?

Deputy Human Resources Minister **Datuk Khairul Firdaus Akbar Khan** told the Dewan Rakyat (Malaysia's lower house) on 7 November 2026 that changes to the **National Wages Consultative Council Act 2011** will make **educational qualifications** part of how mandatory starting pay is set.

He was answering a question on whether firms could re-label degree-level jobs as diploma or SPM jobs to pay less. Once the change is in law, he said, employers cannot ignore a worker's qualifications when deciding their position.

## What are the key numbers?

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Graduate and semi-skilled starting salary | RM2,500 a month (proposed) |
| Expected start | June 2027, with a new minimum wage order |
| Skills-related underemployment (2024) | 32.2% |
| Underemployment target (2030) | 30% |
| Wage target (2030) | RM3,500 |
| Target share of employee pay in GDP (2030) | 40% |

The RM2,500 figure was first announced in **Budget 2027**.

## How will pay be set?

The National Wages Consultative Council is reviewing its starting salary guidelines using the **Malaysia Standard Classification of Occupations**, so that pay reflects both qualifications and skills.

## What about training?

The Department of Skills Development has **2,028** active National Occupational Skills Standards guiding training, and its Akademi Dalam Industri scheme delivers workplace-based training at **1,391 centres**.

## What should graduates do now?

- **Check job titles and duties** in offers made after June 2027.
- **Keep copies of your qualifications** ready for HR.
- **Build skills employers want**, as a higher pay floor may make firms more selective.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Better starting pay** for degree holders from mid-2027.
- **Fewer loopholes**: job downgrades to cut pay should be blocked.
- **Skills mismatch remains**, so practical training still matters.
