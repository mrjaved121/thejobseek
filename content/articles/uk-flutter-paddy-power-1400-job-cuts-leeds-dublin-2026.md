**Flutter Entertainment**, the owner of **Paddy Power**, plans to cut **up to 1,400 jobs** in its **UK and Ireland** business. Staff were told on **Tuesday, 20 October 2026**, by email and on a morning call.

The cuts affect around **15% to 16%** of the division's **roughly 9,000 employees**.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Jobs at risk | Up to 1,400 |
| UK and Ireland workforce | About 9,000 |
| Main locations hit | Leeds and Dublin |
| Other hubs affected | Porto (Portugal), Cluj-Napoca (Romania) |
| Restructuring cost | $80–90 million, expected in Q4 2026 |

Flutter has not said how many of the jobs are in the UK and how many are in Ireland.

## Why the jobs are going

Flutter says the main reason is **higher UK gambling taxes** announced in last year's autumn budget:

| Tax | Change |
|---|---|
| Remote gaming duty (online casino and slots) | Rose from 21% to 40% in April 2026 |
| General betting duty (online bets) | Rising from 15% to 25% in April 2027 |
| Machine games duty | Possible rise at the next Budget (not confirmed) |

Kevin Harrington, who runs Flutter's UK and Ireland business, said the company had warned a year ago that it could not take on a tax rise of this size without cutting fixed costs. He added that Flutter still expects to win market share as the UK's biggest operator.

## Wider picture for UK jobs

The announcement comes at a difficult time. UK vacancies have fallen to their lowest level in 12 years outside the pandemic, and several large employers, including Barclays, have announced cuts this month. Online betting is a major employer in **Leeds**, so local tech and customer service workers may face more competition for roles.

## What it means for job seekers

- **If you work at Flutter,** ask about the consultation process, redeployment options and your redundancy pay. In the UK, large redundancies normally require a consultation period.
- **Tech, data and product workers** from gambling firms have skills that transfer well to fintech, e-commerce and gaming companies. Update your CV to highlight those skills.
- **Customer service staff** should look at banking, utilities and retail contact centres, which often hire people with regulated-industry experience.
- **Job seekers in Leeds and Dublin:** expect more experienced candidates in the market in the coming months, so tailor each application.
