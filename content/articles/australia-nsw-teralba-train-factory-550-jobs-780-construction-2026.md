The **Hunter region** is set to become home to New South Wales' next generation of trains. The NSW Government has named a former **Glencore** mine site at **Teralba**, in Lake Macquarie, as its **preferred location** for a new state-owned train factory, it announced on **20 October 2026**.

The **$12 billion Future Fleet Program** is expected to support **up to 780 construction jobs** and **around 550 ongoing jobs** across the plant and its supply chains.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Project | Future Fleet Program train manufacturing facility |
| Preferred site | Former Glencore mine land, Teralba (Lake Macquarie) |
| Back-up site | Broadmeadow (government-owned) |
| Programme value | $12 billion |
| Construction jobs | Up to 780 |
| Ongoing jobs | Around 550 (factory and supply chains) |
| First fleet | Replacement for Sydney's Tangara trains |
| Manufacturer | Not yet chosen (EOI opened September 2026) |

## Decades of work

The factory is meant to keep building trains for more than **30 years**. After the Tangara replacements, the plan points to replacing the Millennium and OSCAR fleets in the **2040s** and the Waratah trains in the **2050s**. That kind of long pipeline is rare, and it means steady careers rather than short contracts.

Teralba was chosen over Broadmeadow because it is larger and more flexible. It has space for manufacturing, assembly, commissioning, on-site testing of moving trains and future growth.

## Jobs already happening

The government also pointed to work under way now. The **Tangara Life Extension Program**, at the Cardiff rail facility and in Sydney, has taken on **100 new qualified workers** and **20 apprentices**. Domestic Manufacturing Minister Courtney Houssos said each manufacturing job creates roughly three and a half more jobs in the supply chain.

## Likely roles

| Stage | Example jobs |
|---|---|
| Site construction | Civil workers, plant operators, electricians, project managers |
| Train manufacturing | Welders, fitters, electricians, painters, assemblers |
| Testing and commissioning | Rail systems engineers, test technicians |
| Supply chain | Machinists, fabricators, cable and electronics makers |
| Training | Apprentices in engineering and electrical trades |

## How to apply

There is no recruitment open for the factory yet. The site still needs investigations, planning approval and land purchase, and the train maker has not been selected. Watch NSW Government and Transport for NSW announcements, and the careers pages of the manufacturer once chosen.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Hunter trades workers should take note.** Former mining and heavy industry skills transfer well to rail manufacturing.
- **Apprenticeships are a strong route.** Long-life factories usually run regular apprentice intakes.
- **Supply chain firms will hire first.** Local engineering and fabrication companies may win early work.
- **Be patient.** Construction dates are still to be confirmed, so this is a medium-term opportunity.
