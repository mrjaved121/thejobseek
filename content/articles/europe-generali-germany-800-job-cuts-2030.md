Italian insurer **Generali** will cut about **800 jobs in Germany** as part of a restructuring plan, according to a Reuters report on **23 October 2026**. The cuts at **Generali Deutschland** equal about **9%** of its **9,100 employees** in the country and will be spread out until the **end of 2030**.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Company | Generali Deutschland (part of Italy's Generali group) |
| Jobs to be cut | About 800 |
| Current German workforce | 9,100 |
| Share of staff | About 9% |
| Deadline | End of 2030 |
| Main method | Retirements and natural attrition |
| Tech investment | More than €100 million in technology and AI |

## How the cuts will happen

Generali says most of the reduction will come from **staff retiring** and **people leaving who are not replaced**, rather than forced redundancies. Because the timeline runs for more than four years, the yearly impact will be fairly small.

The insurer also plans to **bring some offices together** to save on property costs, as more staff work in a hybrid way.

## The role of AI

Generali will spend **more than €100 million** (about $112 million) on **technology and artificial intelligence**. The company says this will automate routine tasks and make the business more efficient in the long run. That is a pattern seen across European insurance and banking, where claims handling, policy administration and back-office processing are increasingly done by software.

## Germany's job market

Germany has seen a wave of job cut announcements in 2026, especially in car making, chemicals and industry. Financial services had been more stable, but insurers are now also cutting costs. Even so, Germany still has worker shortages in areas such as nursing, skilled trades and IT.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Fewer entry-level admin jobs** in German insurance. Roles based on routine paperwork are most at risk.
- **Tech skills are rising in value.** Insurers are hiring data, IT and AI specialists to build the systems that replace manual work.
- **Generali staff** should ask about internal moves, retraining and early retirement offers before decisions are made.
- **International job seekers:** look at sectors still short of workers, such as health care and skilled trades, rather than insurance back-office roles.
