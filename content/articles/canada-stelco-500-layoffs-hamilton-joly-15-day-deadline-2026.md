The Canadian government has given the owner of **Stelco** **15 days** to reverse plans to lay off up to **500 steelworkers** in Ontario, or face possible legal action. Industry Minister **Mélanie Joly** set the deadline in a letter sent on **22 October 2026** to Stelco, which is owned by US steelmaker **Cleveland-Cliffs**.

Joly said the company is still moving ahead with the layoffs at its **Hamilton** and **Lake Erie** operations.

## What is happening at Stelco

| Detail | What we know |
|---|---|
| Planned job cuts | Up to 500 |
| Plants affected | Hamilton Works and Lake Erie Works (Nanticoke) |
| Union estimate | 300 to 350 layoffs in Hamilton over about three weeks |
| First layoffs effective | 11 October 2026 |
| Lines idled | Cold-rolled and coated operations at Hamilton |
| Government deadline | 15 days from 22 October (early November) |

Stelco has indefinitely idled its cold-rolled and coated steel lines in Hamilton and is moving production to Lake Erie Works. The company blames **US tariffs** and trade disruption. It says demand for these products in its usual markets fell by nearly **25%** in the second quarter of 2026 compared with its 2024 average.

## The job promises at the centre of the dispute

When Cleveland-Cliffs bought Stelco in **2024**, it signed legally binding commitments under the **Investment Canada Act**. In her second letter, Joly cited the job floors for the first time:

- **Unionized workers:** a yearly average of no fewer than **1,766**
- **Salaried staff:** **603**, equal to 90% of the earlier average

These floors are measured as annual averages and do not directly ban layoffs. Stelco says it is still in compliance and has asked Ottawa to relax some commitments, citing serious market disruption. Cleveland-Cliffs declined to comment on the letter.

## What Ottawa could do next

Joly said the letter sets out the government's evidence ahead of a possible court case. Options include asking a court to **force compliance**, seeking **penalties**, or even ordering a **sale of Stelco**, which she said is "definitely on the table".

The fight comes as Canada's job market weakens. The country lost jobs in September and unemployment rose to **6.5%**; see our [September 2026 Canada jobs report](/canada/canada-jobs-report-september-2026-unemployment-6-5/).

## What it means for job seekers

- **Laid-off Stelco workers** should apply for **Employment Insurance** right away. A temporary waiver of the one-week waiting period for tariff-hit workers was extended this year. Our [EI guide](/canada/canada-employment-insurance-ei-new-workers-2026/) explains the basics.
- **Keep in touch with USW Local 1005** for recall news; the duration of the layoffs is not yet known.
- **Skilled tradespeople** such as millwrights and electricians can widen their search to construction, utilities and other manufacturers in the Hamilton–Niagara area.
- **Watch the early-November deadline.** What Ottawa does next may decide whether some of these jobs return.
