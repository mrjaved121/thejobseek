Members of Bahrain's **Council of Representatives** want companies that win **government projects** to fill at least **80%** of their jobs with **Bahrainis**. The proposed legislation, reported by the Gulf Daily News on **24 October 2026**, is due to be debated and voted on at parliament's Tuesday session.

MPs have also tabled a second idea: using **artificial intelligence** to match Bahraini job seekers with vacancies.

## The two proposals

| Proposal | What it would do | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 80% Bahrainisation on state projects | Firms taking government projects would need Bahrainis to make up at least 80% of staff | Due for debate and vote |
| AI job matching | AI would find vacancies, study employer needs and match qualified Bahrainis to roles | Due for debate |

The reports do not yet give details on deadlines, penalties or which kinds of projects would be covered.

## Why MPs want this

Supporters say the measures would **lower unemployment among citizens** and give the authorities better data for decisions about **training**, **recruitment** and **hiring expatriates**. Bahrain has already moved to reserve some fields for nationals, such as a draft law on [private security jobs](/bahrain/bahrain-private-security-jobs-bahrainis-draft-law-2026/).

## What happens next

A parliamentary proposal is only the first step. If MPs approve it, it goes to the government and the Shura Council. Rules are usually followed by an implementing decision that sets dates, scope and exceptions.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Bahrainis:** Contractors on government projects could become a major source of jobs, especially in construction, engineering, IT and services.
- **Bahrainis should register with official employment services** so they are in the system if AI matching goes ahead.
- **Expats:** Companies relying on state contracts may hire fewer foreign staff in future, so specialist and senior skills will matter more.
- **Wait for the final rules.** Nothing changes until the law is passed and published.
