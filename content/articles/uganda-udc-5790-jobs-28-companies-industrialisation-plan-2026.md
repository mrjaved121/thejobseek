State investment is creating real **Uganda jobs**. The **Uganda Development Corporation (UDC)** says the **28 companies** it has backed in the last ten years now employ **over 5,790 people directly**, and it has set out plans for **faster industrial growth**, more local factories and higher exports. UDC shared the update in Kampala on **4 November 2026**.

## What has UDC achieved?

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Companies invested in | 28 |
| Direct jobs created | 5,790+ |
| Period | Past decade |
| Farm produce bought by supported firms | 35,000+ tonnes a year |
| Paid to farmers and local suppliers | About Shs138 billion a year |

UDC executive director **Patrick Birungi** and Trade, Industry and Cooperatives Minister **Sanjay Tanna** spoke at the briefing at the Uganda Media Centre.

## Why does this matter beyond direct jobs?

Because UDC-backed firms buy large amounts of local produce, their impact reaches far beyond their own payrolls. Every tonne bought supports income for farmers, transporters and suppliers across Uganda.

## What are UDC's plans?

UDC's new plan focuses on three goals:

- **Faster industrial growth**
- **More products made in Uganda**
- **Bigger exports**

More factories and processing plants usually mean more jobs in production, logistics and farming supply chains.

## Which jobs could grow?

Industrial and agro-processing investment typically creates roles such as:

- Factory and machine operators
- Quality control and food safety staff
- Maintenance technicians and engineers
- Procurement officers buying from farmers
- Drivers, warehouse and logistics staff

These are typical roles for this kind of investment, not a confirmed vacancy list.

## How do I find these jobs?

Watch for openings at UDC-supported companies and new manufacturing projects announced by UDC and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Cooperatives, and check reputable Ugandan job listings.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Agro-processing is a strong job source** in Uganda.
- **Technical skills** in machinery and food processing are valuable.
- **Farmers benefit too**, through steady demand for their produce.
