The UK Civil Service is one of the country's largest employers. Many people from the Gulf, South Asia and Africa ask whether they can apply. The answer depends on your **nationality** and the **type of post**.

## Two kinds of post

| Type of post | Share of jobs | Who can apply |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-reserved** | About **75%** | UK nationals, **Commonwealth citizens** and **EEA nationals** |
| **Reserved** | About 25% | **UK nationals only** |

**Reserved posts** are those that need a special allegiance to the state, typically in security, intelligence, defence and some diplomatic roles.

If you hold **dual nationality** and one of them is British, you can apply for any Civil Service job.

## Who counts as a Commonwealth citizen?

The rules include an annex listing all Commonwealth territories. Many countries are on it, including **India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Canada and Australia**. Gulf states such as the UAE and Saudi Arabia are **not** Commonwealth members.

## Nationality is not the same as right to work

Meeting the nationality rules does **not** give you permission to work in the UK. You must also have the right to work, for example through **settled status, a Skilled Worker visa or another eligible visa**. Check each job advert for its eligibility and security clearance requirements.

## Where to find jobs

Civil Service vacancies are advertised on the **Civil Service Jobs** website on GOV.UK. Each advert says whether the post is reserved and what nationality requirements apply.

## Tips

- **Check the nationality section of each advert** before applying.
- **Security clearance** can need years of UK residence, which can rule out recent arrivals even for non-reserved posts.
- Civil Service applications use **"Success Profiles"**, which assess behaviours, strengths and experience. Read the job pack carefully.
