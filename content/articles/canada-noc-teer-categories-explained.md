If you are applying to work or immigrate to Canada, one of the first things you need is your **NOC code**. It tells the Canadian government what kind of job you do, and it decides which immigration programs you can use.

## What is the NOC?

The **National Occupational Classification (NOC)** is Canada's system for grouping jobs. Each occupation gets a **five-digit code**. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) uses **NOC 2021 Version 1.0**, which replaced the old NOC 2016 system on **16 November 2022**.

If you see old terms like "skill level A" or "skill type 0", they come from NOC 2016 and are no longer used.

## The six TEER categories

**TEER** stands for **Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities**. Every job is placed in one of six levels:

| TEER | What the job usually needs | Example jobs |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Management roles | Financial managers, marketing managers |
| **1** | A university degree | Software engineers, financial advisors |
| **2** | A college diploma or apprenticeship of 2+ years, or a supervisory role | Medical lab technologists, network technicians |
| **3** | A college diploma or apprenticeship under 2 years, or 6+ months of on-the-job training | Bakers, dental assistants |
| **4** | A high school diploma or several weeks of on-the-job training | Retail salespersons, home child care providers |
| **5** | A short work demonstration, no formal education | Landscaping labourers, delivery drivers |

## Which TEER levels count for Express Entry

| Express Entry program | TEER levels accepted |
|---|---|
| Federal Skilled Worker Program | **0, 1, 2 and 3** |
| Canadian Experience Class | **0, 1, 2 and 3** |
| Federal Skilled Trades Program | Certain **2 and 3** trades only |

Jobs in TEER 4 and 5 are not eligible for these three programs. Compare the programs here: [Express Entry: FSW vs CEC vs FST](/canada/canada-express-entry-fsw-cec-fst-requirements-compared/).

## How to find your NOC code

1. Go to the official **NOC website** and select **NOC 2021 Version 1.0**.
2. Search by your **job title** or a keyword.
3. Open the closest match and read the **main duties**.
4. Check that most of those duties match what you **actually do** at work.
5. Note the **five-digit code**, the title and the **TEER** level.

If the duties don't match, keep looking. IRCC goes by the official NOC website if it disagrees with any other table.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by job title only.** A "manager" title doesn't mean TEER 0 if your duties are mostly hands-on work.
- **Using an old NOC 2016 code.** Make sure the code is from NOC 2021.
- **Reference letters that don't match.** Your employer's reference letter should describe duties that match your NOC code.
