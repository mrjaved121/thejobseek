The United States needs teachers, and many schools are now looking abroad to find them. A new national study shows that **about 1 in 8 teaching jobs in the USA** is either empty or filled by someone who is not fully qualified for it. School districts from Alaska to North Carolina already bring in teachers from other countries, mainly on the **J-1 Teacher visa**.

This guide explains, in simple steps, who can apply, which documents you need, and how to apply safely. It covers teachers in the Gulf as well as in their home countries.

## Is the US teacher shortage real? The numbers

Yes. The **Learning Policy Institute (LPI)**, an independent US education research group, published its 2026 study in July:

| What was measured (2026) | Number |
|---|---|
| Teaching posts that were **empty** (29 states + Washington DC that share this data) | **37,569** |
| Teachers **not fully certified** for the job they are doing (all 50 states + DC) | **387,843** |
| **Total** posts empty or filled by a not-fully-certified teacher | **at least 425,412** (about **1 in 8** teaching jobs) |

LPI says the real number is probably higher, because some states do not report full data. The total has gone up three years in a row.

### Which subjects need teachers most?

According to US Department of Education data used by LPI:

| Subject | States reporting a shortage |
|---|---|
| Special education | 45 states |
| Science | 41 states |
| Maths | 40 states |

Elementary (primary) teaching, language arts (English) and career and technical education also show big gaps. Bilingual and world language teachers are in demand too, because many US students speak another language at home.

## US schools are already hiring teachers from abroad

- In **Halifax County, North Carolina**, **103 of 159** teachers come from other countries, the Associated Press reported in March 2026.
- In **Allendale County, South Carolina**, about **a quarter** of teachers are from abroad, mostly from Jamaica and the Philippines.
- In **Alaska**, there are **573 international teachers**, according to the Alaska Council of School Administrators. In some rural districts, teachers on visas make up half or more of all staff.
- In **Phoenix, Arizona**, the Balsz Elementary School District hired teachers from abroad on **J-1 visas** this year and is looking at more recruiting partners.

## Which visa? J-1 is the main route

| | **J-1 Teacher visa** | **H-1B work visa** |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | US Department of State (exchange programme) | US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) |
| How long | **3 years**, can be extended by 1 or 2 years | Up to 3 years, can be extended |
| How places are given | Through an official **J-1 sponsor** working with the school | A **yearly lottery**. The 2027 cap was already full in July 2026 |
| Best for | Most teachers applying from abroad | Teachers whose school is ready to sponsor them in the next lottery |

**What about the $100,000 H-1B fee?** The US government added a $100,000 fee on new H-1B visas in September 2025. Courts have since blocked it, and the government's appeal is still going on. Rules may change, so most schools hiring from abroad now use the **J-1** route. Read more: [the latest on the $100,000 H-1B fee](/usa/us-h1b-100000-fee-blocked-courts-october-2026/).

## Who can apply? Check yourself

Under the US State Department's J-1 Teacher rules, you must:

- ✅ **Be qualified to teach** at primary or secondary level in your home country, or in the country where you last lived legally (for example, the UAE)
- ✅ **Be working as a teacher** in that country when you apply, *or* have finished a master's or other advanced degree in the last 12 months (with 2 years of full-time teaching in the last 8 years)
- ✅ **Have a degree** equal to a US bachelor's degree in **education** or in **the subject you will teach**
- ✅ Have **at least 2 years (24 months)** of teaching or related work experience
- ✅ **Speak good English.** The sponsor will check this with a test or an interview
- ✅ **Meet the rules of the US state** where you will teach, including background checks
- ✅ Be of good character, with a clean record

The job must be a **full-time teaching job** at an accredited school, where you are the main teacher of the class.

## Documents you will need

Keep clear scans (PDF) of all of these in one folder before you start:

| Document | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| **Passport** (valid for at least 6 months beyond your stay) | Identity and visa |
| **Degree certificate and transcripts (mark sheets)** | Proves your education |
| **Teaching licence or certificate** from your country | Proves you are a qualified teacher |
| **Experience letters** from your schools (with dates, subjects, full-time status) | Proves 2+ years of teaching |
| **Credential evaluation** (a report saying your degree equals a US degree) | US states and sponsors usually ask for this |
| **English test result** (such as IELTS or TOEFL), if the sponsor asks | Proves your English level |
| **Police clearance certificate** | Background check |
| **US-style CV (résumé)**, 1–2 pages | Shows your experience clearly |
| **2–3 reference letters** from principals or supervisors | Shows your work quality |
| **Recent passport-size photo** | Application and visa |

**Tip:** if your documents are not in English, get certified English translations.

## How to apply: step by step

1. **Choose your subject and level.** Shortage areas such as special education, maths, science or primary teaching give you the best chance.
2. **Prepare your documents** from the list above, and get your degree evaluated if you have not already.
3. **Find an official J-1 sponsor or a hiring school district.** Use the State Department's official J-1 Teacher page (button below) and its **sponsor search** to see the list of approved sponsors. Many sponsors and some school districts run international teacher recruitment and post vacancies on their own websites.
4. **Apply online** to the sponsor or school with your CV and documents. Most do **video interviews**, and some ask for a sample lesson.
5. **Get a job offer from a US school.** Read the contract carefully: salary, school, city, start date and who pays which costs.
6. **Receive Form DS-2019** from your sponsor. This is the document you need to apply for the J-1 visa.
7. **Pay the SEVIS fee and the visa fee** (paid to the US government), fill in the online visa form (DS-160), and book an interview at the **US embassy or consulate** in the country where you live.
8. **Get your state teaching permit** with help from your school, then travel before the school year starts. Most US school years start in **August or September**, and hiring for them often happens in the spring.

## Stay safe: avoid fake US teaching job offers

- **Check the sponsor is on the official State Department list** before you share documents or pay anything.
- **Official sponsors must tell you all fees in writing** before you join. Ask for this in writing.
- **Never pay** anyone who promises a "guaranteed" US teaching job or visa, or who asks for money by WhatsApp or bank transfer to a personal account.
- **Your US salary** should be similar to what US teachers at the same school earn. Ask for the exact amount in your contract.

Read: [warning signs of a fake job offer](/uae/uae-job-offer-scam-warning-signs/).

## What it means for teachers in the Gulf and abroad

- **Demand is real and growing.** With hundreds of thousands of posts empty or filled by uncertified staff, qualified teachers, especially in special education, maths and science, have a strong chance.
- **You can apply from where you work now.** A teacher working in the UAE, Saudi Arabia or Qatar can apply if they meet the rules for the country where they live and teach.
- **Plan ahead.** Start collecting documents and your credential evaluation now, so you are ready when sponsors open applications for the next school year.
- **The J-1 is temporary.** It is a 3–5 year cultural exchange, not a green card. After it ends, you normally go home or abroad for two years before you can join the programme again.
