Oman's current Labour Law, issued by **Royal Decree 53/2023** on **31 July 2023**, replaced the old 2003 law. It applies to private sector workers, including expats. Here are the rights that matter most.

## Working hours

- **8 hours a day**, and no more than **40 hours a week**. The old law allowed 9 hours a day.
- Ramadan hours stayed the same as under the previous rules.

## Annual leave

| Rule | What the law says |
|---|---|
| Entitlement | At least **30 days** of paid leave a year |
| When it starts | After **6 months** of continuous service |
| Carry-over | Up to **30 days** into the next year |
| Long break | At least one **30-day** uninterrupted break every **2 years** |
| Air ticket | Non-Omani workers get a **return ticket** home for their leave |
| Unused leave on exit | Paid on your **full wage**, not just basic |

Your employer can postpone your leave by up to six months for business needs. You **cannot give up** your legal leave.

## Sick leave

Paid sick leave can last up to **182 days**:

| Days of sickness | Pay |
|---|---|
| Days 1 to 21 | 100% |
| Days 22 to 35 | 75% |
| Days 36 to 70 | 50% |
| Days 71 to 182 | 35% |

## Family leave

- **Maternity:** **98 days** paid, up from 50 under the old law.
- **Childcare:** up to **one year unpaid**, if requested.
- **Nursing:** a **one-hour** daily break after returning to work.
- **Paternity:** **7 days** paid, to be taken within 98 days of the birth.

## If your job is ending

- During your notice period, you get **10 paid hours a week** to look for a new job.
- If you are **not paid for two months in a row**, you can end the contract **without notice**.

## Omanisation

Employers must meet **Omanisation** quotas, and some jobs are reserved for Omanis. A non-Omani worker can lawfully be replaced by an Omani in the same role. Work visas remain available where there are skills gaps.

## Tips

- Keep your **contract and payslips** safe.
- Raise wage problems with Oman's **Ministry of Labour**.
