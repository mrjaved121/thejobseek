The United States has **permanently ended** the automatic work permit extension that allowed many immigrants to keep working while their renewal was processed. A **final rule** from U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on **14 October 2026**, adopts an October 2025 interim rule **without any changes**.

The rule takes effect on its publication date. For most affected workers, nothing changes in daily practice: the extension already stopped for renewals filed from 30 October 2025. What the final rule removes is any hope that the old system would come back.

## The rule at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Rule | Removal of Automatic Extensions for Certain Employment Authorization Documents |
| Issued by | USCIS, Department of Homeland Security |
| Published | Scheduled for 14 October 2026 (Federal Register document 2026-20955) |
| Effective | On publication |
| Who is affected | EAD renewals filed on or after 30 October 2025 in categories that used to get the extension |
| Not affected | Renewals filed before 30 October 2025; extensions set by law; TPS extensions announced by Federal Register notice |
| Old protection | Automatic extension of up to 540 days while a renewal was pending |

## Who loses the automatic extension

Before October 2025, renewal applicants in 18 EAD categories could keep working for up to 540 days after their card expired, as long as they filed on time. Under the new rule, these applicants need USCIS to **approve the renewal before the current card expires**. Main groups include:

- **H-4 spouses of H-1B workers** (category C26)
- **Green card applicants** with a pending adjustment of status (C09)
- **Asylum applicants** (C08), and refugees and asylees (A03, A05)

Spouses of E and L visa holders (A17, A18) were also on the old list, but law firm guidance notes they are authorised to work through their status, so the change affects them less.

USCIS says the change lets it finish security vetting and screening before granting a new period of work authorisation. The rule's own estimate is that **20,000 to 33,000 EADs** could lapse over an 18-month period starting May 2027.

## What it means for workers

- **A gap in work authorisation is now possible.** If USCIS has not approved your renewal by the time your card expires, you may have to stop working until the new card arrives.
- **Employers will re-verify.** HR teams cannot accept a receipt notice as proof of continued work authorisation for these categories.
- **Timing is everything.** The rule itself notes that the later you file, the more likely a lapse becomes.

## Tips to avoid a gap

1. **File early.** USCIS generally recommends filing an EAD renewal up to **180 days** before the current card expires.
2. **Check the expiry date of every family member's card**, especially H-4 spouses whose status depends on the H-1B holder's extension.
3. **Tell your employer early** if your renewal is still pending close to the expiry date, so you can plan leave if needed.
4. **Check other bases for work authorisation.** Some people, such as certain refugees and asylees, may be authorised to work through their status itself and may need to show other documents.
