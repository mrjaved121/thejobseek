**A £14.7 million project at the Port of Mostyn in Flintshire is expected to support 300 jobs as an old industrial site is brought back into use for the offshore wind industry.** Site clearance at **Marsh Row** is finished, and the next steps are reconnecting services and refurbishing buildings for new business tenants. Ambition North Wales, which runs the North Wales Growth Deal, says the jobs will be split between **213 direct** and **87 indirect** full-time equivalent posts.

## What is happening at the Port of Mostyn?

The port, on the Dee Estuary in North Wales, already plays a big part in offshore wind. Three wind farm operations and maintenance bases work from there, employing about **250 people**.

This new scheme turns disused land and buildings next to the port into space for firms in the renewable energy supply chain. It will also let the port handle bigger offshore wind parts.

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Project | Marsh Row industrial site upgrade |
| Total cost | £14.7 million |
| Growth Deal funding | £4 million (rest from the port) |
| Jobs supported | 300 FTE (213 direct, 87 indirect) |
| Buildings | 172,000 sq ft refurbished by 2030 |
| Employment land | 45 acres back in use by 2032 |
| Location | Mostyn, Flintshire, North Wales |

## Why does offshore wind matter here?

New wind farms are being planned off the North Wales coast. The **Awel y Môr** project recently won a UK Government contract and could see up to **50 turbines** built about 10km offshore. Ports close to these sites need space for storage, assembly and maintenance crews, and Mostyn is positioning itself to win that work.

The upgrade is a separate project from the port's planned **350-metre quay**, which got approval in February 2025.

## Which roles are likely to open?

No job adverts have been published yet. Firms that set up in port-side renewable energy parks usually hire:

- marine and mechanical technicians
- wind turbine service and maintenance staff
- crane operators, riggers and logistics workers
- electrical engineers and fabricators
- warehouse, health and safety and admin staff

These are typical examples for this kind of site, not confirmed vacancies.

## How do I find these jobs?

There is no single application page yet, because jobs will come from the businesses that lease the refurbished buildings. Keep an eye on the Port of Mostyn's announcements, wind farm operators working from the port, and local job listings in Flintshire and Deeside. Training in wind turbine safety (such as GWO courses) can help you get ready.

## What it means for job seekers

- North Wales is building a cluster of offshore wind jobs, with more turbines planned off the coast.
- Jobs will arrive in phases up to 2030 and beyond, so there is time to train.
- Engineering apprenticeships at local colleges are a good way into the sector.
- Supply-chain firms often need welders, electricians and drivers, not only wind specialists.
