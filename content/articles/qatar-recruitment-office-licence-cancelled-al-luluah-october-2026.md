Qatar's **Ministry of Labour** has shut down a licensed recruitment office and cancelled its licence, a reminder that agencies bringing workers into the country can lose their right to operate if they ignore the rules or leave complaints unresolved.

The ministry announced on **Wednesday, 21 October 2026**, that the **Al Luluah (Pearl)** office for recruiting workers, holding **licence No. 638**, had been closed. Local reports describe it as an office recruiting domestic workers.

## The decision at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Authority | Qatar Ministry of Labour |
| Office | Al Luluah (Pearl) recruitment office |
| Licence number | 638 |
| Action | Office closed and licence cancelled |
| Legal basis | Minister of Labour Decision No. 47 of 2026 |
| Announced | 21 October 2026 |
| Complaints hotline | 16505 |

## Why the licence was cancelled

The ministry gave three reasons:

- **Breaking the rules** that govern recruitment activity and services.
- **Not following decisions** issued by the ministry.
- **Failing to cooperate** in settling complaints from its clients.

No fines were mentioned. The ministry said the step is meant to tighten compliance and protect the rights of people who use recruitment services, and that it will keep acting against offices that break the rules.

## What it means for workers and households

- **Clients with open cases** at this office should contact the ministry rather than wait for the office to respond.
- **Workers recruited through an agency** keep the protections of Qatar's labour rules, including the [minimum wage of QAR 1,000 plus allowances](/qatar/qatar-minimum-wage-1000-riyals-allowances/) and on-time salary payment.
- **Complaints matter.** The case shows the ministry treats unresolved complaints as a reason to act against an office, so it is worth reporting problems formally.

## Tips before you use a recruitment office in Qatar

1. **Ask for the licence number** and confirm it with the Ministry of Labour before paying anything.
2. **Get a written contract** that states the fees, the timeline and what happens if the placement fails.
3. **Pay through traceable methods** and keep every receipt.
4. **Report problems early** by calling 16505, and keep copies of messages with the office.
5. **Workers should never hand over money** to secure a job. If someone asks you for recruitment fees, treat it as a warning sign, as explained in our guide to [spotting fake Gulf job offers](/uae/uae-job-offer-scam-warning-signs/).
