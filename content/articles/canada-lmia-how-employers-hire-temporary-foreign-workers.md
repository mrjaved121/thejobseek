Many Gulf-based workers are offered "LMIA jobs" in Canada. To understand those offers, and to spot fake ones, it helps to know how the **Temporary Foreign Worker Program** works from the employer's side.

## What is an LMIA?

A **Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA)** is a document a Canadian employer gets from **Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC)** before hiring a foreign worker. A **positive LMIA** shows the job needs a foreign worker because no Canadian or permanent resident was available.

With a positive LMIA and a job offer, the worker then applies to **Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC)** for a **work permit**.

## The fee: $1,000, paid by the employer

- The LMIA processing fee is **$1,000 per position**.
- The **employer must pay it**. It can **never be charged to or recovered from the worker**.
- It is not refunded if the application is withdrawn or the LMIA is negative.

> If anyone asks you to pay for an "LMIA job", walk away. That is not allowed and is a common scam.

## Employers must advertise first

Before applying, employers must try to hire locally:

| Requirement | High-wage jobs | Low-wage jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment activities | At least **3** | At least **3** |
| Job Bank advert | Required | Required |
| Other methods | At least **2**, one national | At least **2**, one national |
| How long the advert runs | **4 weeks** in a row, within the 3 months before applying | **8 weeks** in a row, within the 3 months before applying |

Whether a job is high-wage or low-wage depends on whether the offered wage is at or above the provincial or territorial wage threshold.

## Extra rules for low-wage jobs

- **Cap:** low-wage foreign workers can be at most **10%** of the staff at a work location, or **20%** in some sectors such as construction, food manufacturing, hospitals and nursing homes.
- **High-unemployment cities:** since **26 September 2024**, low-wage LMIAs are not processed in cities with an unemployment rate of **6% or more**.
- **Transport:** the employer pays the worker's **round-trip travel**.
- **Housing:** must be suitable and **affordable**, costing less than **30%** of the worker's before-tax income.
- **Health insurance:** the employer pays for private emergency cover until provincial health coverage starts.

## What workers are entitled to

- Wages at least equal to the **prevailing wage** for the job.
- A **signed employment agreement** on or before the first day of work, matching the job offer.
- **Full-time work**, meaning at least **30 hours a week**.
- Workplace safety insurance at no cost to the worker.

## High-wage jobs: the transition plan

For high-wage jobs, employers must also submit a **transition plan** showing how they will recruit, train and retain Canadians and reduce reliance on foreign workers.

## Tips for job seekers

- **Never pay** for an LMIA or a job offer.
- **Check that the employer is real.** Look up the company and its official contact details yourself.
- **Read the offer carefully.** It should state the job title, NOC code, wage, hours and duties.
