Parcel firm **Evri** is signing up new **self-employed couriers** across the UK for its Christmas rush, and eligible starters can collect **up to £2,200 in bonuses**. Weekly top-ups of **£200 to £250** begin after five delivery days and run until **January 2027**. You can apply through the "Be a courier" page on Evri's official website.

## Why is Evri recruiting now?

The weeks from Black Friday to Christmas are the company's busiest. Evri, which handles more than one billion parcels a year, expects to move **over four million parcels a day** at the peak. Local papers from Glasgow to Carlisle reported the same drive on 9 November, showing it is a nationwide push rather than a single depot.

## How much can an Evri courier earn?

Couriers are paid per parcel delivered. Evri says new couriers can make **more than £100 for each delivery day**, and the seasonal bonus is added on top. Once a round is finished, the rest of the day is free, which is why the work is popular with parents, students and people with another job.

## What other support is offered?

- Daily **fuel support payments**
- Perks via **Onsi**, such as getting part of your pay early, GP appointments and shop discounts
- The **Go Electric** scheme to help couriers move to electric vans or cars
- **Owner-driver** roles through the DHL eCommerce UK network, which merged with Evri last year

## Who can apply?

Because the roles are self-employed, you will usually need your own vehicle, insurance that covers business deliveries, a smartphone and the right to work in the UK. Check the exact conditions on Evri's site before you sign up.

## How do I apply?

Go to Evri's official [Be a courier page](https://www.evri.com/careers/be-a-courier), enter your postcode and choose the delivery days you can commit to. Apply early: rounds are allocated before the November peak builds.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Quick extra income** before Christmas, with bonuses paid weekly.
- **Flexible days** that fit around school runs or another job.
- **Know your costs:** as a self-employed courier, fuel, insurance and tax come out of your earnings.
