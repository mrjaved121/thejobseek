UAE companies outside the oil industry **added staff again in September 2026**, after cutting back in August. The **S&P Global UAE PMI**, released on **5 October**, held at **55.3**, matching August's 20-month high. But the rise in jobs was described as **modest**.

## Key numbers

| Indicator | UAE | Dubai |
|---|---|---|
| Headline PMI (September) | 55.3 (unchanged) | 54.5 (up from 54.1) |
| Employment | Rose slightly after August fall | Rose slightly after August fall |
| New orders | Strong; exports best in about two years | Marked rise; exports best in two years |
| Prices charged | Fastest rise since May 2011 | Fastest rise since January 2014 |
| Outlook | Near recent lows | Just above March's low |

## Why hiring is careful

S&P Global economist **David Owen** said the non-oil economy has moved past the **mid-year slowdown** linked to the Middle East conflict. Orders are back, especially from abroad. But firms are still **uncertain about the outlook**, so they are slow to take on permanent staff.

In Dubai, staff costs barely changed. Some firms raised pay, while others held back on hiring.

## What else the survey shows

- **Work is piling up.** Backlogs grew again, which often leads to more hiring if it continues.
- **Costs are high.** Oil price swings and shipping problems are pushing up input costs, and firms are passing them on.
- **Suppliers are faster.** Delivery times shortened in Dubai for a fourth month.

This lines up with recruitment data showing [UAE hiring rebounded 4% in Q3 2026](/uae/uae-hiring-rebounds-4-percent-q3-2026-cooper-fitch/).

## What it means for job seekers

- **Openings are returning.** Firms with growing order books are the best targets now.
- **Expect careful offers.** Employers may prefer contract or project roles before committing to permanent hires.
- **Export-focused firms are busier.** Trading, logistics and manufacturing companies selling abroad reported strong demand.
- **Negotiate with care.** Pay rises are uneven, so research salaries for your role before talks.
