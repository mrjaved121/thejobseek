Canada's job market weakened for a second straight month. According to **Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey**, released on **9 October 2026**, employment fell by **68,000** in September and the unemployment rate rose to **6.5%**.

## Key figures for September 2026

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Change in employment | −68,000 (−0.3%) |
| Full-time / part-time | −35,000 / −33,000 |
| Unemployment rate | 6.5% (up 0.1 points) |
| Employment rate | 60.6% |
| Average hourly wage | $37.64, up 2.3% on a year earlier |
| Youth unemployment (15–24) | 13.0% |

This followed a drop of 42,000 jobs in August.

## Which sectors and provinces

- **Losses:** educational services (−35,000), health care and social assistance (−23,000) and manufacturing (−13,000).
- **Gains:** "other services" added 17,000 jobs.
- **Provinces:** jobs fell in **Quebec** (−49,000) and **British Columbia** (−20,000), while **Alberta** added 23,000, and its unemployment rate dropped to 6.4%.

## What it means for people planning to move to Canada

- **A softer job market makes job offers harder to get,** especially in education and health care this month.
- **Alberta is the bright spot.** Job growth there was the strongest of any province in September.
- **Express Entry is changing on 3 November 2026,** with extra points for Canadian experience or job offers in high-wage occupations. Read: [Canada changes Express Entry on 3 November](/canada/canada-express-entry-changes-november-2026-high-wage-occupations/).
- **Be wary of "guaranteed job offers"** sold for a fee. Real employers do not charge candidates.
