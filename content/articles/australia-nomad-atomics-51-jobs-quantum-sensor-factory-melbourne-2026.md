A young Australian quantum technology company is bringing its manufacturing home. The Victorian Government announced on **21 October 2026** that **Nomad Atomics** will create **up to 51 new jobs** as it builds a **Sovereign Manufacturing Facility** in Melbourne.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Employer | Nomad Atomics |
| Location | Richmond, Melbourne, Victoria |
| New jobs | Up to 51 |
| Timeline | Over the next three years |
| Project | Sovereign Manufacturing Facility for quantum sensors |
| Support | Victorian Government |
| Announced by | Melissa Horne, Minister for Industry and Advanced Manufacturing |

## About the company

Nomad Atomics was founded by **three postdoctoral physicists**. It builds **quantum sensors** and pairs them with software to give detailed information about what is under the ground and in the surrounding environment. CEO and co-founder **Kyle Hardman** says the firm's equipment has already set world firsts for sensing below the surface.

The company moved its headquarters from **Canberra to Richmond in 2024**, where it set up laboratories. Until now, parts of its manufacturing have been done overseas. The new facility will let it make its sensing equipment in Victoria instead.

## Why it matters

The state government says the expansion will strengthen research and development, raise productivity and help the company break into overseas markets. Minister Horne said the support is about creating local jobs and keeping Victoria at the front of advanced manufacturing and innovation.

Quantum technology is still a small field in Australia, so 51 jobs in one company is a meaningful step for people with physics, engineering and precision manufacturing skills.

## Jobs likely to be in demand

The government has not published a list of roles. For a quantum hardware maker scaling up production, likely jobs include:

- Physicists and quantum research scientists
- Optical, laser and electronics engineers
- Precision manufacturing and assembly technicians
- Software and embedded systems developers
- Data scientists working with sensor data
- Quality, supply chain and operations staff

## What it means for job seekers

- **STEM graduates should take note.** Physics, electrical engineering and mechatronics graduates in Melbourne have a new local employer to watch.
- **Technicians are needed too.** Manufacturing sensors needs skilled assembly, testing and calibration staff, not only PhDs.
- **Hiring will be gradual.** The jobs will be added over three years, so follow the company on LinkedIn and its official website.
- **Build hands-on skills.** Experience with lasers, vacuum systems, electronics or clean-room work is valuable in this field.
