Dublin-born AI company **Tines** will create **100 new jobs** in the Irish capital over the next **two years**, and **more than half** of them will be **entry-level**. The plan was reported on **12 October 2026**, during the International AI Summit held in Dublin.

That makes it one of the more useful tech announcements for graduates this autumn, at a time when many firms are hiring mainly experienced staff.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Employer | Tines |
| New jobs | 100 |
| Entry-level share | More than half |
| Timeline | Next two years |
| Location | Dublin (new headquarters opening later in 2026) |
| Founded | 2018 |
| Also based in | Boston, USA |

## Which roles are open

| Team | Typical starting roles |
|---|---|
| Engineering | Software engineers, graduate developers |
| Product | Product managers, designers |
| Sales | Sales development and account roles |
| Marketing | Content, events and growth roles |
| Customer success | Onboarding and support for business clients |

The company describes the roles as a direct way for graduates and early-career people to get into the **AI sector**.

## About the company

Tines builds a platform that lets staff create and run software and AI tools inside a secure company system. Clients include **SAP, Mars, Reddit, Canva, Fin and Headspace**. It became a "unicorn", worth over $1 billion, in early 2025 and has raised **$272 million** from investors including Goldman Sachs, SoftBank and Accel.

Earlier this year it added 100 jobs in the US with a new Boston hub. The Dublin plan matches that, and the new hires will support growth of its latest product, Tines 3B.

For newcomers, the appeal is that a fast-growing company tends to give junior staff real responsibility early, and the skills learned (AI tools, automation, security workflows) carry over to many other employers.

## How to apply

Vacancies are posted on the company's official careers page at [tines.com/careers](https://www.tines.com/careers). Choose Dublin as the location.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Graduates have a real shot.** With most roles at entry level, a strong degree project or internship can be enough.
- **Sales and customer success are good routes.** These teams often hire people without a computer science background.
- **Show automation skills.** Building a simple workflow or automation project gives you something concrete to talk about.
- **Combine applications.** Several Dublin tech firms are hiring at once, so apply widely.

Another Dublin tech firm growing fast is [Whatnot, which plans to double its Irish team](/europe/europe-whatnot-dublin-double-irish-workforce-european-hq-2026/).
