A major investment plan could create **Ghana jobs** over the next four years. President **John Dramani Mahama** says the government will put **more than $9 billion** into **job-rich sectors** between **2027 and 2030** under a policy called the **New Economy**, with full details due in the **2027 budget on 12 November 2026**.

## What did the President announce?

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Policy | New Economy |
| Investment | More than $9 billion |
| Period | 2027 to 2030 |
| Details due | 2027 budget, 12 November 2026 |
| Presented by | Finance Minister Cassiel Ato Forson |
| Where announced | Hogbetsotso Festival, Anloga, Volta Region, 7 November |

## Which sectors will benefit?

For the **Volta Region**, Mahama named:

- Textiles and garment making
- Commercial agriculture
- Hospitality and tourism

In October he had described a wider **New Economy** framework covering nine productive areas, including **pharmaceuticals** and **agribusiness**, 3News reported.

## What questions remain?

The policy think tank **IERPP** has asked the government to publish how the plan will be financed, how projects will be chosen and how money will be split between sectors. Watch the budget for those answers.

Finance Minister Ato Forson has also said the government wants the programme to help Ghana move towards an **investment-grade credit rating**; it is currently rated **B**.

## Which jobs could grow?

If the money flows as planned, the named sectors typically create roles such as:

- Garment machinists and factory supervisors
- Farm managers, agronomists and agro-processing staff
- Hotel, restaurant and tour operations staff
- Quality control and logistics workers

These are typical roles for these industries, not announced vacancies.

## How should job seekers prepare?

- **Follow the 12 November budget** for sector-by-sector plans.
- **Build skills** in agriculture, textiles or hospitality.
- **Watch regional programmes**, especially in the Volta Region.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Big public investment** is planned for job-heavy sectors.
- **Details still to come**, so treat timelines with care.
- **Regional opportunities** may open outside Accra.
