Social media posts and some news sites in South Asia are sharing the headline that the UK has "freed" skilled workers from their employers. **That is only partly true.** The change is real, but it covers a specific group of workers. Here is what the UK government actually announced, and what every Skilled Worker visa holder needs to know about changing jobs.

## What actually changed

The Home Office announced on **9 October 2026** that Skilled Workers who are **officially recognised as victims of modern slavery** are no longer tied to their sponsoring employer. Once recognised, they are free to do **any legal job** until their visa ends. The rule change took effect under the Immigration Rules from **8 October 2026**.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who benefits? | Skilled Workers with a positive **"conclusive grounds"** decision that they are a victim of modern slavery |
| What can they do? | Walk away from the abusive sponsor and work legally for anyone, or for themselves, until the visa expires |
| Who is excluded? | Professional sportspeople and sports coaches |
| Does it apply to every Skilled Worker? | **No.** Other workers still need a new sponsor and a visa update to change employer |

Before this, a worker who walked away from an abusive sponsor could lose both their visa and their income, because Skilled Worker visa holders cannot claim most public benefits. The Home Office gave the example of a care worker who paid an agency **£20,000** for a job, was then asked by her sponsor for **£400 a month** to keep her visa, and was later paid as little as **£200–£300 a month**.

## The crackdown on bad sponsors

The government says more than **6,600 sponsors** have had their licence to hire overseas workers taken away since it came to power. Over **4,400** of those were in the year to June 2026, up **140%** on the previous year. Sponsors suspected of crimes are referred to the police.

The government has also said in its Immigration White Paper that it intends to **make job moves simpler for sponsored workers** in future. No date or details have been announced yet.

## If you are a normal Skilled Worker: how to change employer legally

For everyone else, the standard rules still apply. According to GOV.UK:

1. **Get a job offer and a new certificate of sponsorship (CoS)** from a licensed UK sponsor. The new job must meet the Skilled Worker rules, including salary.
2. **Apply online to update your visa.** You can apply up to **3 months** before your new start date.
3. **Keep working in your current job** while you wait, including your notice period, as long as you applied **before your current visa expires**.
4. **Do not start the new job** until your new permission is confirmed.
5. **Stay within the Common Travel Area** (the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man) while you wait. A trip abroad before the decision means your application is withdrawn.
6. Expect a decision within about **8 weeks**.

You also need to update your visa if you move to a **different occupation code** with the same employer.

## Second jobs: the 20-hour rule

| Situation | Do you need a visa update? |
|---|---|
| Overtime in your sponsored job | No, there is no overtime limit |
| Second job up to **20 hours a week** that meets the rules (higher-skilled eligible occupation, on the immigration salary list, or same sector and level as your main job) | No |
| Second job of **more than 20 hours a week** | Yes, with a CoS from the second employer and a letter explaining the change |
| Unpaid volunteering for a registered charity or voluntary body | No |

## What it means for workers in the Gulf planning a UK move

- **Choose your first sponsor carefully.** Changing employer still means a new CoS and a new application, so check that the employer is licensed and genuine.
- **Never pay for a CoS or a "visa job".** Paying an agent for sponsorship is a common route to exploitation, as the Home Office's own example shows.
- **Keep records** of your contract, payslips and messages. If you are being exploited, report it to the police. Recognition as a victim is what unlocks the new freedom to change employer.
- English level **B2** is now required for new Skilled Worker applicants. See: [UK Skilled Worker English requirement](/uk/uk-skilled-worker-visa-english-b2-requirement/).
