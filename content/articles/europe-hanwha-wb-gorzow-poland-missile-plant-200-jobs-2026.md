New **Europe jobs for foreigners** and locals are coming to western Poland. South Korea's **Hanwha Aerospace** has broken ground on a **guided missile plant in Gorzów Wielkopolski**, which is expected to create **more than 200 jobs**. **Hanwha WB Advanced Systems**, a joint venture between Hanwha and Poland's **WB Group**, will operate it.

## What is being built?

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Operator | Hanwha WB Advanced Systems (Hanwha 51%, WB Electronics 49%) |
| Location | Gorzów Wielkopolski, western Poland |
| Site size | About 1.16 million sq m |
| Jobs | More than 200 |
| Product | CGR-080 guided missiles (about 80 km range) for the Homar-K launcher |
| Completion | 2028 |
| Production | Initial in late 2029, full-scale in 2030 |

It will be Hanwha Aerospace's **first guided missile factory outside South Korea**. Hanwha may later make missiles of other ranges there and wants Poland to become its European missile hub.

## Why does this matter for jobs?

Besides direct jobs, the venture plans to **buy parts and materials from local suppliers**, which supports more work across the region. Poland is also receiving production technology and know-how under a licensing agreement.

## What jobs could be available?

Defence factories of this kind generally recruit for:

- Mechanical, electrical and systems engineers
- Production and assembly technicians
- Quality assurance and testing specialists
- Supply chain and logistics staff
- Health, safety and security roles

Hanwha has not yet listed actual vacancies, so treat these as examples. Some defence jobs may require Polish citizenship or a security clearance.

## How do I apply?

Recruitment details have not been announced. Watch the official websites of Hanwha Aerospace and WB Group for openings in Gorzów Wielkopolski as the 2028 completion date approaches.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Engineering skills are in demand**, especially in electronics and systems.
