Part-time work is growing in the UAE. In the last two months, **Sharjah** has opened part-time government jobs to retirees and senior citizens, and the federal pension authority has started covering **Emiratis who work part-time**. Here is what is new, who it applies to, and what the rules mean for **expat workers** looking for part-time jobs.

## 1. Sharjah's part-time scheme for retirees and seniors

On **7 September 2026**, the **Ruler of Sharjah, Sheikh Dr Sultan bin Muhammad Al Qasimi**, approved a plan that lets these groups take **part-time jobs in Sharjah's public sector**:

- senior citizens
- their spouses
- retired people

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Work pattern | Flexible, such as **three days a week** or set hours only |
| Type of work | Light roles, such as checking visitor tickets at Sharjah's museums |
| Aim | Better quality of life, keeping seniors active, and family financial stability |
| Pay, start date, how to apply | **Not yet announced** |

According to reports, the Ruler said some people could work as little as one or two hours a day, depending on what they can manage. A large number of retirees were reported to be interested after the announcement.

The same directive told Sharjah's HR department to speed up hiring **1,200 Emiratis**, towards a target of **3,000 UAE citizens** in the emirate's public sector.

## 2. Pension cover for Emiratis working part-time

On **21 August 2026**, the **General Pension and Social Security Authority (GPSSA)** announced that Emiratis working part-time in the **public or private sector** are covered by the UAE pension system.

| Rule | Details |
|---|---|
| Who counts as part-time | **8 to 32 hours a week**, or **1 to 4 days a week** (20% to 80% of full time) |
| Employer duty | Register eligible part-time Emirati staff with the GPSSA, and update the **Maashi** platform if someone moves from full-time to part-time |
| How workers join | Ask your HR department to register you |
| Benefits | Pension or end-of-service gratuity, disability pension and survivors' pension, worked out in proportion to hours |

This makes part-time work a safer choice for Emiratis who want to study, care for family or ease into retirement.

## 3. Part-time work for expats: the rules

Part-time contracts have been allowed for everyone, nationals and residents, since the UAE's new labour law took effect in **February 2022**. For expats, the key points are:

- **You need a part-time work permit** from the **Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE)**. It allows fewer hours and work for **more than one employer**, and is valid for **2 years**.
- **Total hours are capped.** Even with several jobs, you cannot work more than **48 hours a week** in total.
- **Your contract sets your hours.** On a part-time permit, you cannot be made to work more hours than your contract says unless you agree in writing.
- **Visit visas don't count.** You cannot take any job, full-time or part-time, on a visit visa.
- **Tutoring has its own permit.** Private lessons need MoHRE's free **private tutoring work permit**.

Read more: [UAE work permit types explained](/uae/uae-work-permit-types-mohre-13-explained/) and [UAE working hours and overtime rules](/uae/uae-working-hours-overtime-pay-rules/).

## What this means for job seekers

- **Emirati retirees in Sharjah:** watch for official Sharjah Government announcements on how to apply, since the application process has not been published yet.
- **Emiratis in any sector:** if you work part-time, check with HR that you are registered with the GPSSA.
- **Expats:** part-time work is legal, but only through a proper MoHRE permit. Be careful with "part-time job" adverts on social media that ask for fees. See: [Warning signs of a fake UAE job offer](/uae/uae-job-offer-scam-warning-signs/).
