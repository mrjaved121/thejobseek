Regional towns in southern New South Wales are set for a jobs boost, a welcome piece of **Australia hiring 2026** news for people who want to work outside the big cities. On **27 October 2026**, Regional NSW Minister **Tara Moriarty** announced **$50 million** for **20 business projects** in the southern Murray-Darling Basin. Together they are expected to **create and retain 624 jobs**.

## Funding at a glance

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Total funding | $50 million |
| Projects | 20 |
| Jobs | 624 created and retained |
| Region | Southern Murray-Darling Basin, NSW |
| Program | Sustainable Communities Program – Business and Industry Round ($160 million) |
| Funded by | Australian Government |
| Delivered by | NSW Government |

## Projects named so far

| Business / body | Town | Project | Grant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yurunga Farms | Blighty | New modern dairy, raising output from 18.4 to up to 32 million litres a year | $4.91m |
| Wentworth Shire Council | Buronga–Gol Gol | Water infrastructure for a growing corridor | $3.75m |
| Crighton's Engineering | Hay | New purpose-built depot replacing two sites | $2.46m |
| Quincey Jones Jelly Preserves Co. | Moama | New cooking, packing and storage equipment | $176,731 |

The other 16 projects are spread across the region.

## Why the money is being spent

The program helps farming communities adjust as the federal government buys back water for the environment. The aim is to help local businesses grow so towns keep their jobs. Minister Moriarty said NSW does not support water buybacks, but wants to make sure local industries have the infrastructure they need to stay competitive.

Note that the 624 figure covers both **new jobs and existing jobs kept**, so not every position will be a new vacancy.

## Jobs likely to come up

- **Dairy and farm work:** milkers, farmhands and machinery operators as the Blighty dairy expands
- **Engineering and trades:** fabricators, welders and mechanics in Hay
- **Food manufacturing:** production, packing and warehouse roles in Moama
- **Construction:** builders, plumbers and labourers for the new facilities and water works

## How to apply

There is no central application page. Contact the businesses directly or watch local job boards, council websites and regional newspapers in the Riverina and far west of NSW.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Regional work can pay off:** lower living costs and less competition for roles than in Sydney.
- **Farm experience helps:** dairy and agriculture employers value hands-on skills and machinery licences.
- **Overseas workers:** some regional employers take part in skilled and regional visa programs, so ask employers directly.
- **Get your tickets:** forklift, white card and first aid certificates make you job-ready for construction and factory roles.
