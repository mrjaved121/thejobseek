India's diplomatic missions in the UAE, its embassy in Abu Dhabi and its consulate in Dubai, have warned UAE residents to be careful with **private visa and migration agencies** that sell jobs and residency abroad. The advisory, reported by The National on **16 October 2026**, follows what the consulate called "a large number of complaints".

Most complaints are about agencies advertising **jobs or residency in Europe** that never came through after people paid. The warning applies to any job seeker in the UAE, not only Indian nationals.

| Extra risk | Face images and fingerprints misused for bank accounts, loans and credit cards |

## How the scams work

1. **Paid job or residency offers.** Ads on social media promise work or residency in Europe or the UAE in return for a fee. The job never arrives.
2. **"Fixer" services.** Agencies claim they can arrange exit permits, Emirates ID or status regularisation for a fee, mostly targeting low-income workers.
3. **Biometric theft.** Agents collect fingerprints and face scans "for processing". These can then be used to open bank accounts or take loans in the victim's name. Victims can end up with fines, travel bans or even jail.

A consulate representative said that in most cases, people had sent the money from India.

## What it means for job seekers

- **No real employer sells a job.** Dubai Police have also warned that genuine employers do not ask candidates to pay fees or transfer money during recruitment.
- **Europe work visas are applied for through official channels.** Check the rules on the official website of the country, or with its embassy, before paying anyone.
- **Never hand over biometrics to an agent.** Fingerprints and face scans should only be taken at official visa centres or government offices.

## Tips to stay safe

- Do not trust offers that promise **guaranteed approval** or **urgent processing**.
- Do not share passport copies, bank PINs or passwords with unknown agents.
- Do not pay through links on unverified websites.
- If you are scammed in Dubai, report it on the **Dubai Police eCrime platform** or call **901**.
