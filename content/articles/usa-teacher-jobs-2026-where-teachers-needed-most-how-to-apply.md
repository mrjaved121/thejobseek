Schools across the United States are hiring teachers, and in many places they cannot find enough of them. A national study published in July 2026 found that **at least 425,412 teaching jobs**, about **1 in 8**, were either **empty** or filled by a teacher who was **not fully certified** for that job.

This guide shows **where in the USA teachers are needed most**, **which subjects** are hardest to fill, **what teachers earn**, and **exactly how to apply**, step by step.

## The big picture

The **Learning Policy Institute (LPI)**, a US education research group, collects shortage data from every state each year. Its 2026 report found:

| What was measured | Number |
|---|---|
| Teaching posts that were **empty** (29 states + Washington DC that share this data) | **37,569** |
| Teachers **not fully certified** for the job they are doing (all states + DC) | **387,843** |
| **Total** posts that need a qualified teacher | **at least 425,412** |

This is the **third year in a row** that the total has gone up.

## Latest news from the districts (2026–27 school year)

- **North Carolina:** **2,009 K–12 teacher vacancies** as of 21 September 2026, including about **569 special education** ("exceptional children") posts, according to the state's superintendents' association. Elementary (K–5) vacancies went **up**.
- **Florida:** the teachers' union counted **3,157 advertised teaching vacancies** on 10 August 2026, hundreds more than a year earlier. **Broward County** listed **358** teacher openings and **Palm Beach County 276**. The Florida Department of Education expects **nearly 6,500 vacancies** for 2026–27, with **special education (nearly 2,000)** and **elementary education (1,591)** the biggest needs, followed by maths, English, science and ESOL.
- **Nevada (Las Vegas):** the **Clark County School District**, one of the largest in the US, started the school year on 10 August 2026 with **167 vacancies**, **91 of them in special education**.
- **Mississippi:** the state reported **6,907 vacancies** for teachers, administrators and support staff in November 2025, up by 1,747 in a year. **Special education and elementary education** make up **half** of teacher vacancies, with the **Delta** region hit hardest.

**Tip:** if you can teach **special education, maths or science**, you will find the most openings and often extra pay or bonuses.

## How much do US teachers earn?

According to the **National Education Association (NEA)**, for the 2024–25 school year:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Average starting salary** (USA) | **$48,112** |
| **Average teacher salary** (USA) | **$74,495** |
| Highest average salary | California, **$103,552** |
| Lowest average salary | Mississippi, **$54,975** |
| Highest starting salary (state) | Washington, **$60,658** |
| Lowest starting salary (state) | Montana, **$36,682** |

Each district publishes its own **salary schedule**. Pay rises with experience and higher degrees, and most jobs include health insurance and a pension plan.

## Who can apply? Three types of job seekers

### 1. You already have a US teaching licence
You can apply straight away. If you are moving to another state, check that state's rules. Most states have agreements to accept other states' licences, but this is **not automatic**: you may get a temporary licence first and need extra tests or courses. Many shortage districts, including Clark County in Nevada, say they accept most out-of-state licences in the same subject.

### 2. You have a bachelor's degree but no teaching licence
Many shortage states offer an **alternative certification** route. You start teaching and get paid while you train. **Texas** is a good example. According to the Texas Education Agency (TEA), you:

1. Choose the grade and subject you want to teach.
2. Join a **state-approved alternative certification programme** (the TEA website has the list).
3. Meet the programme's entry rules (for example, grades and subject knowledge).
4. **Get hired** by a school as an **intern teacher** and get a one-year **probationary certificate** (background check required).
5. Finish your training and tests (many programmes take about one year) while you teach with a mentor.
6. Apply for your **standard certificate**.

Other districts run their own routes. For example, Clark County School District in Las Vegas has an **"Alternate Route to Licensure"** programme.

**Note:** you must be **legally allowed to work in the USA** (for example, a US citizen, green card holder or someone with a valid work permit) to take a US school job directly.

### 3. You are a teacher living outside the USA
Most schools hire overseas teachers through the **J-1 Teacher exchange visa**, run by the US State Department. You need a teaching degree, at least two years of experience and good English. Some districts, including Clark County, run their own exchange visitor teacher programmes. Read our full guide: [How foreign teachers can apply for US teaching jobs, step by step](/usa/usa-teacher-jobs-for-foreigners-shortage-how-to-apply-j1-2026/).

## How to apply: step by step

**Step 1: Choose your state and subject.** Use the tables above. States with many empty posts, and subjects such as special education, maths and science, give you the best chance.

**Step 2: Check the licence rules on the state's official website.** Every state's Department of Education explains who can teach. For example:
- Texas: [tea.texas.gov](https://tea.texas.gov/texas-educators/preparation-and-continuing-education/becoming-a-certified-texas-educator-through-an-alternative-certification-program) (alternative certification)
- Florida: [fldoe.org/teaching/certification](https://www.fldoe.org/teaching/certification/pathways-routes/teacher-edu-program-graduates.stml)
- Arizona: [azed.gov/educator-certification](https://www.azed.gov/educator-certification/educator-certification-frequent-requests)

For other states, search "*[state name] Department of Education teacher certification*".

**Step 3: Prepare your documents.** Make clear PDF copies of:

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Résumé (CV)** | 1–2 pages: education, teaching experience, subjects and grades taught |
| **Cover letter** | Short and specific to the district and subject |
| **College transcripts and degree** | Unofficial copies are usually fine when you apply |
| **Teaching licence** (or proof you are eligible) | Or your alternative certification programme letter |
| **Test scores** | For example Praxis or your state's teacher exams, if you have taken them |
| **2–3 references** | Principals or supervisors who have seen you work |
| **ID and proof you can work in the USA** | Needed when you are hired |
| **Background check / fingerprints** | The district tells you how to do this after an offer |

**Step 4: Find the openings.** Look in three places:
1. **The school district's own careers website.** Search "*[district name] careers*" or "*[city] public schools jobs*". For example, Clark County School District posts teacher jobs at **jobs.ccsd.net**.
2. **State education job boards.** For example, Nebraska's Department of Education runs **Teach in Nebraska** (nebraskaeducationjobs.ne.gov).
3. **Education job boards and job fairs**, such as **EDJOIN**, which many districts in California and Nevada use.

**Step 5: Apply online.** Create a profile on the district's application system, upload your documents, and apply for each post. You can usually apply to several schools at once.

**Step 6: Interview.** Many districts do a phone or video interview first, then an interview with the school principal. Some ask you to teach a short **sample lesson**.

**Step 7: Accept the offer and complete the paperwork.** This includes the background check, licence or intern certificate, and payroll forms. Then you start teaching.

**When to apply:** most hiring happens from **spring to late summer** for school years that start in **August or September**. Shortage districts also hire **all year round**, including for January starts, so do not wait.

## Stay safe

- **Apply only on official district or state websites.** US public schools do **not** charge you to apply or to be hired.
- **Be careful of "agents"** who promise a guaranteed US teaching job for money.
- **Check the job is real:** it should appear on the district's own website, and emails should come from the district's official domain.

## What it means for job seekers

- **The demand is real.** Hundreds of thousands of US classrooms need a qualified teacher, and the number has grown three years in a row.
- **Go where the shortage is.** Arizona, Mississippi, Illinois, Nevada, Florida, North Carolina and Texas have the most need, and rural districts often have fewer applicants.
- **Pick a shortage subject.** Special education, maths and science teachers have the most choice and often receive bonuses.
- **No licence yet? You can still start.** If you have a bachelor's degree and can work legally in the USA, look at your state's alternative certification route.
