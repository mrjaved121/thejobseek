Dreaming of the Moon? **NASA** is recruiting a new class of **astronauts** to support its **Artemis** lunar missions, and applications close on **3 December 2026**. Applicants must be **US citizens** with a college degree and professional experience; apply through NASA's official astronaut pages and USAJOBS.

## What did NASA announce?

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Employer | NASA (US federal government) |
| Job | Astronaut candidate, 25th class |
| Application deadline | 3 December 2026 |
| Final selection | Expected by late 2027 |
| Training starts | By early 2028 |
| Active astronauts today | 32 |

NASA unveiled the recruitment drive at an air show at Kennedy Space Center. Speaking to the Associated Press, **Randy Bresnik**, who commands Artemis III, said there is more work than NASA has astronauts for.

## How competitive is it?

Very. NASA's previous call, two years ago, attracted **more than 8,000 applicants**, and **10** were picked in September 2025. The new group will be the agency's 25th class, counting from the Mercury Seven of 1959.

## Who can apply?

- **US citizens** only
- A **college degree**
- **Professional work experience**

NASA's own astronaut pages list the full degree, experience and medical requirements, so read them before you start.

## What happens after selection?

New candidates face around **two years of demanding training**, and it may be several more years before a first launch. Artemis III's **Frank Rubio**, who holds the US record of 371 days in space in a row, said the trip to the Moon is only a tiny part of the job; most of the work supports the mission from the ground.

NASA plans to land astronauts on the Moon again in **2028**, the year the new class should begin training.

## How do I apply?

Start on NASA's official **[astronaut page](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/astronauts/)**, which links to the USAJOBS vacancy. Submit before **3 December 2026**.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Rare chance**: NASA only recruits astronauts every few years.
- **Strong STEM backgrounds win**: engineering, science, medicine and flight experience.
- **Not a US citizen?** Look at NASA contractors and other space agencies instead.
