**OpenAI** will create **200 more jobs** in Ireland, the company and **IDA Ireland** announced on **12 October 2026**. The new roles add to **250 jobs** announced in July, meaning OpenAI plans **at least 450 new roles** in the country. It currently employs **over 120 people** there.

The news was announced during the International AI Summit in Ireland.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| New jobs announced | 200 |
| Earlier announcement (July 2026) | 250 |
| Total new roles | At least 450 |
| Current Irish staff | 120+ |
| Location | EU headquarters, Dublin |
| New office | Tropical Fruit Warehouse, from December 2026 |

## Which roles are being filled

Many of the jobs will be in a new **Applied AI Lab** in Dublin, which helps businesses put AI to work. Roles include:

| Area | Example roles |
|---|---|
| Applied AI Lab | Forward deployed engineers, solutions engineers, privacy engineers |
| Commercial | Sales (go-to-market) roles |
| Customer | User operations roles |

## More than jobs

OpenAI also announced support for Ireland's wider tech scene:

- A **"Founder Cafe"** with Dogpatch Labs to train about **150 start-up founders** in ChatGPT, Codex and the API
- **SME Academy** events around Ireland, starting in **Cork in November 2026**
- Continued backing for the **Patch** programme for young builders aged **16 to 21**

Ireland's government welcomed the investment as a sign of confidence in the country's talent and economy.

## How to apply

OpenAI lists all open positions on its official careers page at [openai.com/careers](https://openai.com/careers). Filter by location to see roles in Dublin.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Strong engineers are the main target.** Experience building software for customers, working with APIs, or in data privacy will stand out.
- **Not only coders.** Sales and user operations roles are open too, especially for people with tech or SaaS experience.
- **Show real AI work.** A portfolio of projects using AI tools or APIs is more convincing than a list of courses.
- **Europe-based applicants:** EU citizens can work in Ireland without a permit, which makes Dublin one of Europe's most accessible tech hubs.
