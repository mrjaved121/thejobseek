**Volvo Cars** will cut about **2,500 jobs** worldwide, with roughly **2,000 of them in Sweden**, after the carmaker fell to a loss in the third quarter. The company announced the plan with its results on **23 October 2026**, Reuters reported.

This is the second big round of cuts in two years. After **Håkan Samuelsson** returned as chief executive in 2025, Volvo Cars announced **3,000** job cuts and a major savings plan.

## How the cuts will happen

Volvo Cars said the 2,500 reductions will come from three routes:

1. **Ending contracts with consultants**
2. **Natural attrition**, meaning people who leave are not replaced
3. **Targeted exit packages** offered to employees

The company has not said which departments or job types are affected. It also plans to save another **5 billion Swedish crowns** in indirect and variable costs by the end of 2027.

## Why Volvo Cars is struggling

| Measure | Q3 2026 | Q3 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted operating result | Loss of SEK 3.7 billion (≈ $373m) | Profit of SEK 5.8 billion |
| Gross margin | 14.7% | 20.4% |
| China revenue | Down almost 50% | — |

The company pointed to several pressures:

- **Strong competition** from Chinese carmakers
- **Tariffs** and weak demand, including in the US
- **Slower demand for electric cars**
- **High costs**, including for developing new models

Samuelsson called the quarter "very disappointing" and said the turnaround plan needs strengthening. Earlier in October, the company withdrew its full-year forecasts for sales volume and cash flow. Volvo Cars is majority-owned by China's **Geely Holding**.

## Europe's car industry under pressure

Volvo Cars is the latest European carmaker to shrink its workforce as electric car sales slow and Chinese rivals gain ground. Volkswagen and BMW have both announced large job cuts in Germany this year. ## What it means for job seekers

- **Consultants and contractors** working for Volvo Cars in Sweden are likely to feel the impact first.
- **Employees offered exit packages** should compare the offer with their notice and severance rights under Swedish rules and talk to their union before signing.
- **Expect more competition** for automotive and software jobs around Gothenburg, where Volvo Cars is based.
- **Look beyond cars.** Engineering and software skills transfer well to sectors such as energy, industrial technology and transport.
