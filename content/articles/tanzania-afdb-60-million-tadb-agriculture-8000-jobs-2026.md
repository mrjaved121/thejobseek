New **Tanzania jobs** in farming and food businesses could follow a major finance deal. A **$60 million loan** from the **African Development Bank Group** will add capital to the **Tanzania Agricultural Development Bank (TADB)** as part of a **$120 million** plan targeting **8,000 jobs**, mainly for **women and young people**. It was announced on 4 November 2026.

## What has been approved?

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Lender | African Development Bank Group |
| Loan | $60 million to Tanzania, invested as equity in TADB |
| Government contribution | $60 million (from capitalised retained earnings) |
| Total | $120 million |
| Project | TADB Equity Enhancement Project, phase III |
| Jobs target | 8,000 |
| Approved | 21 October 2026 |

## Who will the jobs help?

At least **30%** of the supported jobs are meant to benefit **women**, and another **30%** **young people**. The project also focuses on **climate-resilient farming**: TADB wants green farm loans to make up **12%** of its lending by 2030, up from just **3%** in 2025.

## How will the money create jobs?

Stronger capital lets TADB lend more to farmers and agribusinesses that struggle to get long-term credit. It plans to expand:

- Direct loans to farm businesses
- Wholesale funding to other lenders
- Co-financing with partner banks

Jobs are expected to come from expanding farms, processing plants, irrigation, storage and distribution businesses. The 8,000 figure is a **target**, so real results will depend on how the loans are used.

## Which jobs could grow?

Agricultural finance projects like this typically support work in:

- Crop and livestock farming
- Food processing and packaging
- Storage, cold chain and transport
- Irrigation and farm machinery services
- Agro-dealer and input supply businesses

These are typical areas, not confirmed vacancies.

## How do I benefit?

Farmers and agribusiness owners can approach TADB and its partner banks for financing. Job seekers should watch for openings at expanding agribusinesses and processing companies in their region.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Agriculture remains Tanzania's biggest employer**, and more finance means more growth.
- **Women and youth are priority groups** under this project.
- **Agri-processing and logistics** skills will be useful as the sector modernises.
