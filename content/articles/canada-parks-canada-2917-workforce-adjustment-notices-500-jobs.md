**Parks Canada** has told **2,917 employees** that their jobs are affected by a new round of cuts. That is about **61%** of the **4,773 people** the agency employed at the end of March 2026. The agency plans to cut the equivalent of around **500 full-time positions**.

Chief executive **Andrew Campbell** announced the second phase of cuts in an email to staff on **20 October 2026**, and letters went out during the week.

## Key facts

| Detail | Figure |
|---|---|
| Employees receiving "affected" letters | 2,917 |
| Total Parks Canada staff (31 March 2026) | 4,773 |
| Share of workforce | About 61% |
| Full-time positions to be cut | About 500 |
| Savings required | $144.9 million over 3 years |
| First round (June 2026) | 15 full-time employees affected |

## Why the cuts are happening

Ottawa's **Comprehensive Expenditure Review** asks federal departments and agencies to reduce spending. Parks Canada's share is **$144.9 million over three years**. A spokesperson said decisions on who will lose their jobs are expected **within weeks**.

## Who is affected

The **Public Service Alliance of Canada (PSAC)** says **2,671 of its members** at the agency got letters, and that most of the jobs at risk are **on-site roles** at national parks, national historic sites and canals. The union says the agency has not explained which programmes or services will be reduced.

PSAC has also raised concerns about staffing at busy parks and about the roughly **375 workers** rebuilding **Jasper** after the 2024 wildfire.

## What an "affected" letter means

- It is **not a layoff notice**. It warns that a job could be cut, changed or moved.
- A later **"opting" letter** begins the process that may end in layoff.
- Under the PSAC collective agreement, affected staff can use **alternation**, trading jobs with a co-worker who is ready to go.
- Parks Canada says an affected letter does not automatically end employment or staff housing.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Fewer federal openings:** with spending cuts across the public service, expect fewer new government postings in the coming months. Check the official [GC Jobs](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/opportunities/government.html) site for what remains.
- **Seasonal park work** may be harder to find in 2027. Look at provincial parks, tourism operators and private outdoor businesses.
- **If you received a letter,** speak to your union and HR about your options before making any decision.
- **Broaden your search.** Canada lost jobs in September, so private-sector competition is also rising.
