Good news for anyone searching for **NHS jobs**: the number of qualified clinical staff working in the NHS in England grew by **13,047 full-time equivalents (FTE)** in the year to August 2026. NHS England published the figures on **29 October 2026**, and they show clinical hiring is still growing even though the wider workforce shrank slightly.

## How many clinical staff does the NHS now employ?

| Measure | August 2026 | Change in a year |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified clinical staff (FTE) | 758,351 | +13,047 (+1.8%) |
| Qualified clinical staff (people) | 839,858 | +14,415 |
| Total NHS staff (FTE) | — | down almost 4,000 |

FTE measures working capacity. One FTE equals one full-time post, so two half-time nurses count as one FTE.

## Which jobs count as "qualified clinical"?

The group covers the frontline professionals that patients see every day:

- Doctors
- Qualified nurses and health visitors
- Midwives
- Scientific, therapeutic and technical staff (for example physiotherapists, radiographers, pharmacists and lab scientists)
- Qualified ambulance staff

## Why did overall staffing fall?

While clinical numbers rose, the total NHS workforce fell by almost 4,000 FTE. That means the reduction came from non-clinical areas, such as some support, admin and management roles, as trusts balance budgets against rising patient demand. For job seekers, the message is clear: **clinical skills are where the demand is**.

## How do I apply?

All NHS trusts in England (and NHS Wales) advertise on the official **[NHS Jobs website](https://www.jobs.nhs.uk/candidate)**. You can search by role, band and location, set up job alerts and apply online. Many trusts also say on the advert whether they can offer a Skilled Worker visa, so check each listing carefully.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Clinical roles are the safest bet.** Nurses, midwives, allied health professionals and paramedics remain in demand.
- **Register with your regulator early.** Nurses and midwives need NMC registration; doctors need GMC registration; many therapy roles need HCPC registration.
- **Look beyond London.** Trusts in the Midlands, North and coastal areas often find it hardest to recruit.
- **Non-clinical roles are tighter.** Admin and management hiring is slowing, so expect more competition there.
