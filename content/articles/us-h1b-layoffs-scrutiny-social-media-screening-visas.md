US authorities are looking harder at **H-1B employers that cut staff** and at the **online activity of visa applicants**. Two separate measures are now in force.

## 1. Layoffs now count in H-1B cases

**Executive Order 14431**, signed on **18 September 2026**, tells the **Departments of State, Labor and Homeland Security** to consider whether a sponsor "directly or indirectly engaged in layoffs within the previous year" when they review a Labor Condition Application, petition, visa or entry. Planned layoffs that affect similar US workers also count.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Order | Executive Order 14431, 18 September 2026 |
| Agencies | State, Labor, Homeland Security |
| What is checked | Layoffs in the previous year and planned layoffs |
| Labor Department action | Review of past LCA data within 30 days |
| Ban on sponsorship? | No |
| Existing petitions revoked? | No |

On **8 October 2026**, the Labor Department also suspended eight companies from the PERM green card process, citing sponsorship during layoffs.

## 2. Social media screening covers more visas

| Visa types | Since |
|---|---|
| F, M, J (students and exchange visitors) | June 2025 |
| H-1B and H-4 | 15 December 2025 |
| I, TN and TD | 1 October, according to the State Department |

Applicants are asked to set all social media profiles to **public** so consular officers can review them.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Ask about layoffs.** If your sponsor recently cut staff, expect more questions and possible delays.
- **Check your online profile.** Make sure your public posts and CV details match your visa application.
- **Plan for longer processing.** Extra reviews can slow interview scheduling.
