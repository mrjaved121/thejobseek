Defence technology group **Thales Australia** has opened a new **sonar manufacturing facility** in **Rydalmere, Western Sydney**, and expects it to create **dozens of engineering and advanced manufacturing jobs** over the coming years. The site was opened on 26 October 2026 by Defence Industry Minister **Pat Conroy**, and is a notable **Australia hiring 2026** story for engineers and technicians in New South Wales.

## About the facility

The new **100-metre manufacturing hall** sits within the Thales Australia **Acoustics Centre of Excellence**. It was paid for jointly by Thales and the Commonwealth Government in a multimillion-dollar investment; the exact figure was not given.

## What will be made there

The hall is designed to build sonar systems from start to finish and to service them locally. Products include:

- **Towed array sonars** for the Royal Australian Navy's **Hunter Class frigates**
- Sonar systems for **Collins Class submarines**
- **BlueSentry** autonomous surveillance systems
- **Thin-line arrays** for uncrewed surface vessels

Thales plans to supply both the Australian Navy and export customers. Building and maintaining these systems at home also means more long-term sustainment work in Sydney.

## Jobs to expect

Thales describes the new roles as **engineering and advanced manufacturing** positions. Likely roles include:

- **Electronics and acoustic engineers**
- **Mechanical and systems engineers**
- **Production technicians** and **assemblers**
- **Test and quality engineers**

Local suppliers of parts and services are also expected to gain work.

## A note on eligibility

Defence work in Australia often requires **Australian citizenship** and the ability to obtain a **Defence security clearance**. Read each advert carefully. Some engineering and commercial roles at Thales may have different requirements, but foreign job seekers should not assume visa sponsorship is available for this programme.
