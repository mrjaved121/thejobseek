**Mars is investing £161 million in its pet food factory in Melton Mowbray, Leicestershire, and says the first year will create more than 30 skilled engineering and technical jobs and raise its apprenticeship group to 33 places.** The plan was announced on 10 November 2026 and runs to 2031. Vacancies are posted on the [Mars UK careers site](https://careers.mars.com/uk/en).

## What is Mars investing in?

The money will pay for fast new production lines that make recyclable pet food pouches, newer manufacturing technology and upgrades that cut waste. Cameras using artificial intelligence will spot quality problems earlier.

When the work is done, Melton Mowbray will be the company's main UK factory for pouched pet food. Mars has made pet food in the town for 75 years, and Whiskas and Pedigree are produced there.

## Which roles are open?

Mars describes the new posts as skilled technical and engineering roles. The apprenticeships cover technical and operational areas, with **mechanical and electrical engineering** named directly.

Plant manager Sharon Ross said the company wants to give the next generation engineering skills that are ready for the future. The exact job titles and start dates were not published in the announcement, so check the careers page for live adverts.

## Who can apply?

No special rules were given. For engineering apprenticeships in England, employers usually ask for GCSEs in maths, English and science. Experienced roles normally need a relevant engineering qualification and time spent in a factory setting. These are general guides, not Mars requirements.

Leicestershire County Council says it is bringing partners together so that local people, suppliers and contractors can benefit.

## How do I apply?

- Visit the [Mars careers website for the UK](https://careers.mars.com/uk/en).
- Search for "Melton Mowbray" or filter by engineering and apprenticeship roles.
- Apply online; Mars does not ask applicants for payment.

If nothing suitable is listed today, set up a job alert, as roles will open in stages.

## What it means for job seekers

- The numbers are modest, but these are well-paid skilled posts at one of the area's biggest employers.
- Apprenticeships let school leavers earn while training as engineers.
- Food factories across the UK are automating, so maintenance, electrical and controls skills are valuable.
- Work will continue for five years, which means more contracts for local trades and suppliers.
