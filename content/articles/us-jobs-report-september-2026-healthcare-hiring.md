US hiring stayed slow in September. The **Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)** reported on **2 October 2026** that employers added only **29,000 jobs**, and the unemployment rate held at **4.2%**.

## Key figures

| Measure | September 2026 |
|---|---|
| Jobs added (nonfarm payrolls) | +29,000 |
| Average monthly gain, past 12 months | +45,000 |
| Unemployment rate | 4.2% |
| Unemployed people | 7.1 million |
| Average hourly earnings | $37.81 (up 0.1% on the month, 3.0% on the year) |

## Where the jobs were

- **Health care:** +17,000, led by outpatient care (+13,000) and hospitals (+12,000)
- **Construction:** +11,000
- **Manufacturing:** +9,000

Jobs fell in **financial activities** (−7,000) and in **nursing and residential care facilities** (−9,000).

## What it means for overseas professionals

- **Health care is still the strongest sector** for hiring, including roles that can lead to US work visas or green cards for nurses and other clinicians.
- **Overall hiring is slow,** so competition for sponsored roles in other fields is likely to be tougher.
- **H-1B workers should watch a new proposal** that could make visa extensions harder. Read: [USCIS proposes ending "deference" for H-1B and L-1 extensions](/usa/uscis-proposes-ending-deference-h1b-l1-extensions/).
- **Apply through employers' own careers pages** and be wary of anyone charging fees for a US job or visa.
