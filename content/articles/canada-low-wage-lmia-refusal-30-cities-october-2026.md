Canada has widened its freeze on **low-wage foreign worker hiring**. Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) updated its quarterly list on **9 October 2026**, and Service Canada will now refuse to process low-wage Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) applications in **30 census metropolitan areas** where unemployment is **6% or higher**.

The list applies to applications submitted from **9 October 2026 to 7 January 2027**. ESDC will publish the next update on **8 January 2027**.

## The update at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who decides | Employment and Social Development Canada / Service Canada |
| Rule | Low-wage LMIAs are not processed in metro areas with 6%+ unemployment |
| Period | Applications submitted 9 October 2026 – 7 January 2027 |
| Restricted metro areas | 30 |
| Open metro areas | 11 |
| Newly restricted | Halifax, Fredericton, Kingston, St. Catharines-Niagara, Regina, Lethbridge |
| Reopened | Kamloops, Chilliwack |
| Next update | 8 January 2027 |

## Where low-wage LMIAs are still processed

These 11 metro areas are below the 6% line, according to the official ESDC table:

| Province | Metro area (unemployment rate) |
|---|---|
| Quebec | Saguenay (3.7%), Drummondville (3.8%), Sherbrooke (4.2%), Québec (4.3%), Trois-Rivières (5.7%) |
| British Columbia | Kamloops (3.6%), Chilliwack (5.6%), Victoria (5.8%) |
| Ontario | Thunder Bay (4.6%) |
| Manitoba | Winnipeg (5.9%) |
| New Brunswick | Saint John (5.5%) |

Work locations in smaller towns that are **not** part of a census metropolitan area also remain eligible.

## Where they are blocked

All major cities are on the restricted list, including **Toronto (7.5%), Montréal (7.2%), Vancouver (7.0%), Calgary (6.4%), Edmonton (7.6%) and Ottawa-Gatineau (7.9%)**. The highest rates are in Oshawa (9.8%), London (9.1%) and Kelowna (8.6%). Alberta and Saskatchewan have no open metro areas at all.

## Jobs that are exempt

Even in a restricted city, ESDC still processes low-wage LMIAs for:

- Primary agriculture occupations
- Construction, food manufacturing, hospitals, and nursing and residential care facilities
- Certain in-home caregiver jobs in private households (NOC 31301, 32101, 44100 and 44101)
- LMIAs that only support a permanent residence application
- Truly short jobs, generally 120 days or less, with a written exemption request

## What it means for job seekers

- **City matters more than ever.** A low-wage job offer in Toronto or Calgary cannot get an LMIA right now unless it falls in an exempt sector. The same job in Winnipeg or Québec City can.
- **The date of submission counts.** The rate that applies is the one in force on the day the employer submits the LMIA, so timing around the January update can change the outcome.
- **High-wage jobs are not affected.** If the offer pays at or above the provincial wage threshold, it goes through the high-wage stream instead.
- **Watch for fake offers.** Anyone promising a low-wage LMIA job in a restricted city outside the exempt sectors is offering something the system will not process.
