School bus maker **Blue Bird Corporation** will create **375 new jobs** in **Fort Valley, Georgia**, Governor **Brian Kemp** announced on **21 October 2026**. The company plans to spend about **$300 million** turning a former diesel motorhome factory into a new bus plant.

Blue Bird has built buses in Peach County for decades and already supports **more than 1,900 jobs** in the state. The new site is meant to lift output as US school districts replace ageing fleets.

## Key facts

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Employer | Blue Bird Corporation |
| New jobs | 375 over three years |
| Investment | About $300 million |
| Site | 401 Wanderlodge Way, Fort Valley, Peach County |
| Size | Around 1 million sq ft |
| Products | Type C school buses (diesel, gasoline, propane, electric) |
| Construction start | Expected Q1 2027 |

## Which jobs are on offer

The state says Blue Bird will recruit for three broad groups:

| Area | Typical work |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Assembly, welding, painting, wiring and fitting |
| Operations | Materials handling, logistics, planning and supervision |
| Technicians | Maintenance, electrical and quality testing, including electric buses |

Because the plant will build electric as well as fuel-powered buses, people with **electrical or EV training** may find extra openings. Georgia Quick Start, the state's free workforce training programme, is a partner in the project.

## Timeline

Building work is due to begin in **early 2027**, and the 375 jobs will be added in stages over about **three years**. That means most hiring will happen from 2027 onward, but Blue Bird regularly hires at its existing Fort Valley operations.

## How to apply

Blue Bird posts its openings on its official careers page: [blue-bird.com/careers](https://www.blue-bird.com/careers). You can apply now for current roles at the existing Georgia site and set up alerts for the new plant.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Start with the current plant.** Getting hired at Blue Bird's existing Fort Valley site now can lead to a move into the new factory later.
- **Electrical skills pay off.** Electric school buses need technicians who understand high-voltage systems and wiring.
- **Use free training.** Georgia Quick Start courses are often tied to the exact jobs a new plant needs.
- **Stable, long-term work.** School bus demand is driven by public budgets, which tends to keep production steady.
