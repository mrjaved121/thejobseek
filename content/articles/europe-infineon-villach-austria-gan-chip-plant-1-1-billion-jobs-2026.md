High-tech **Europe jobs for foreigners** are getting a long-term boost in Austria. Chipmaker **Infineon Technologies** will invest about **€1.1 billion** in its **Villach** site in Carinthia by 2034, building the world's first high-volume factory for **gallium nitride (GaN) power semiconductors on 300mm wafers**. Production is planned to start at the **end of 2027**.

## What is Infineon building?

GaN chips are much more energy-efficient than standard silicon chips. They are used in AI data centres, power grids, electric cars and robotics. Larger 300mm wafers produce about **2.3 times as many chips** per run as 200mm wafers, which cuts costs.

## What does it mean for jobs?

Infineon has not given a figure for new jobs. Austria's Economics Minister **Wolfgang Hattmannsdorfer** said each semiconductor job can support **up to six more** at suppliers, logistics and service firms. Infineon Austria CEO **Sabine Herlitschka** said the project will secure highly skilled jobs in the long term.

Infineon Austria's Villach site has grown from 24 employees in 1970 to a global centre for power electronics.

## Which jobs does a chip plant need?

Semiconductor factories typically hire:

- Process, equipment and yield engineers
- Cleanroom operators and maintenance technicians
- Materials scientists and R&D engineers
- Quality, safety and facilities specialists
- IT, data and automation experts

These are typical roles for a chip plant, not a confirmed vacancy list.

Search vacancies on the official **[Infineon careers site](https://www.infineon.com/careers)** and filter by Austria or Villach.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Chip skills are in demand across Europe.** The EU is funding new plants under the European Chips Act.
- **German helps.** Many Austrian roles require German, though some engineering jobs use English.
- **Non-EU applicants** usually need Austria's Red-White-Red Card for skilled work.
