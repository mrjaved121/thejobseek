Oman wants its technology sector to create **3,000 direct jobs every year** until the end of the decade. The target is part of the **second phase of the National Digital Economy Programme**, launched in Muscat on **22 October 2026** under the patronage of Finance Minister **Sultan bin Salim al Habsi**.

The new phase runs from **2026 to 2030**. It also aims to attract **RO300 million** in technology investment and lift the digital economy's contribution to **RO1.5 billion** by 2030.

## A shift from government to business

The first phase (2021–2025) focused on putting government services online. Phase two turns to companies and productive sectors. Its main areas are:

- **Digital trade** and e-commerce
- **Electronic payments**
- **Artificial intelligence**
- **Technology that raises business productivity**

A separate **Digital Commerce Programme** will help Omani firms, especially small and medium businesses, sell more widely and use AI for marketing, stock control, demand planning and supply chains. Plans for AI data centres of up to **500 MW** were also mentioned, though no builders, costs or timelines were given.

## Omanisation in tech is rising fast

Figures shared at the launch show more Omanis are working in technology:

| Measure | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Omanisation in tech occupations | 50% | 72% |
| Omanisation in tech-related activities | 33% (2022) | 46% (2026) |
| Tech jobs created for Omanis | — | 5,000+ in five years |
| E-commerce value | RO221m (2023) | RO288m (2025) |
| Fibre subscriptions | 184,000 (2021) | 357,000 (2025) |

Officials also said **251 small companies** have won government technology contracts and **48 incubators and accelerators** have been set up.

## Who is likely to benefit

The 3,000-a-year target is a national goal, not a list of open vacancies. The jobs are expected to come from private companies, start-ups and investors in the sectors above. With Omanisation in IT already at 72%, Omani graduates are the main focus, while experienced specialists may still be needed in areas where local skills are short. ## What it means for job seekers

- **Omani graduates in IT, data and business** should focus on AI, cloud, cyber security, payments and e-commerce skills.
- **Small tech firms and start-ups** winning government contracts are a growing source of entry-level jobs.
- **Expat tech professionals** should expect most general IT posts to go to citizens and should target senior or niche roles.
- **Follow the Ministry of Transport, Communications and IT** for training and placement schemes linked to the programme.
