**The Canadian Immigrant Fair in Mississauga takes place tomorrow, Friday 13 November 2026, from 10am to 3pm at the Living Arts Centre, and it is free to attend.** Newcomers can meet hiring employers and recruiters face to face, have a resume reviewed and sit in on career talks. Register on the organiser's own page before you go; nobody needs to pay or use an agent to get in.

## What is the event?

[INsauga reports](https://www.insauga.com/job-fair-will-connect-newcomers-to-canada-with-mississauga-employers/) that the fair is run by Canadian Immigrant magazine and is built around three themes: careers, education and settlement. Employers, colleges, career advisers and settlement agencies will share one hall.

The same report quotes Statistics Canada figures that explain why such events matter. Almost a third of recent working-age immigrants (31.7 per cent) struggled to land a first job in Canada. Among them, 42.2 per cent pointed to missing Canadian experience or references, and 38.3 per cent said they had no contacts in the job market. A fair is one of the few places where you can start building those contacts in a single day.

## What can I do there?

The [organiser's event page](https://www.canadianimmigrant.ca/events-section/career-fair/mississauga) lists these activities:

- Meeting employers, recruiters and immigrant service agencies
- A free resume clinic and career coaching
- An AI Lab on using artificial intelligence for your resume, LinkedIn profile and job hunt
- Free LinkedIn headshots
- Stands for education and training providers, banks and settlement support

The speakers' programme includes a session on Canada's changing immigration landscape with lawyer Elizabeth Long from 11am to 11:30am, a beginner's talk on AI for job searching by career coach Murali Murthy, an introduction to the CELPIP English test from 1pm to 1:30pm, and a panel called "The Newcomer Career Plan B" about changing fields and building income.

One limit to know: the pages we read do not name the employers who will be recruiting or the number of vacancies. Treat the day as a chance to meet recruiters and improve your application, not as a promise of a job offer.

## How to do it — step by step

1. **Register today.** Go to the [official Mississauga registration page](https://www.canadianimmigrant.ca/events-section/career-fair/register-mississauga) and save your spot. INsauga says sign-up is handled through Eventbrite.
2. **Prepare a Canadian-style resume.** Keep it to two pages, leave out your photo, age and marital status, and put results before duties. Print 10 to 15 copies.
3. **Write a 30-second introduction:** your profession, years of experience, strongest skill and the kind of role you want.
4. **Plan your route.** The venue is at 4141 Living Arts Drive in central Mississauga. Arrive near 10am, when recruiters are fresh and queues are short.
5. **Visit the resume clinic first,** then walk the employer stands with the improved version in mind.
6. **Collect names and emails.** Ask each recruiter how they prefer to receive applications.
7. **Follow up within 48 hours.** Send a short thank-you message with your resume attached and apply on the company's own careers page if they tell you to.

## Tips that improve your chances

- **Translate your experience.** Explain overseas job titles in Canadian terms and mention any licence or credential assessment you have started.
- **Ask good questions,** such as which roles are open now and what the hiring timeline is.
- **Dress as you would for an interview.** Some recruiters hold short screening chats on the spot.
- **Common mistakes:** handing out a five-page CV, having no clear target role and never following up.

Checklist: registration confirmation, printed resumes, pen and notebook, charged phone with LinkedIn ready, work permit or status details in case a recruiter asks.

## A word on scams

Entry is free and employers at a genuine fair do not charge candidates. If anyone at or around the event asks for money for a job offer, a work permit or a "guaranteed placement", walk away. Apply only through the employer's official website or the contact a recruiter gives you in person.

- International students can use the day for networking and resume feedback as well as job leads.
