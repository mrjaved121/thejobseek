Expatriates in Saudi Arabia have been told by the **Ministry of Interior** not to **work for themselves** in breach of the Kingdom's residency and labour rules. The warning, reported in late August 2026, is a reminder of how heavy the penalties are for informal side jobs and self-employment.

## The penalties

| Penalty | Up to |
|---|---|
| Fine | **SAR 50,000** |
| Prison | **6 months** |
| Deportation | Possible, depending on the violation |

The exact penalty depends on the case.

## What counts as "working for yourself"?

In general, it describes an expat on a work visa who earns money outside the job their permit covers, without official approval. Typical examples include:

- running an unlicensed business or shop;
- freelancing or taking side jobs for customers directly;
- doing driving, delivery or repair work on their own account.

Any extra or flexible work must go through **official channels** and your employer. If your registered profession is wrong, fix it the official way. Read: [Saudi work permit correction deadline](/saudi-arabia/saudi-work-permit-correction-deadline-end-2026/).

## How to report violations

The ministry asks the public to report residency, labour and border violations:

| Region | Number |
|---|---|
| Makkah, Madinah, Riyadh, Eastern Province | **911** |
| All other regions | **999** |

Reports are handled confidentially, according to the ministry.

## What it means for expat workers

- **Only work for your sponsoring employer**, in the job on your work permit, unless an official route allows otherwise.
- **Turn down "easy money" side jobs.** A few extra riyals is not worth a SAR 50,000 fine and a ban.
- **Check your status** on Qiwa and Absher to make sure your profession and employer details are correct.
- If you want to change jobs, use the official transfer process through your new employer and Qiwa.
