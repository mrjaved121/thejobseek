The number of **Omanis with disabilities** in work rose by **10%** in 2025 to **18,437**, according to figures from the **National Centre for Statistics and Information (NCSI)** published on **19 October 2026**. Almost half of them, **47%**, work in the **private sector**.

## Key numbers

| Indicator | 2025 |
|---|---|
| Employed Omanis with disabilities | 18,437 (+10%) |
| Share working in the private sector | 47% |
| Economic participation rate (15+) | About 38% |
| Largest field of work | Agriculture, livestock, poultry and fishing (18%) |
| Total Omanis with disabilities | 75,432 (2.5% of Omanis) |
| Share under 40 | 59% |

## Where the jobs are

The biggest single group, **18%**, work in **farming, animal husbandry, poultry and fishing**. Others are spread across government, retail, services and offices. The strong private-sector share shows companies are increasingly including people with disabilities in their teams.

## Support available

Oman pays a disability benefit to more than **51,000** people and funds rehabilitation for over **9,000** people across **128 centres**. Services such as assistive devices are also provided. These programmes help people prepare for and stay in work.

## Room to grow

With **59%** of Omanis with disabilities under the age of 40, there is a large group of working-age people. A participation rate of about **38%** shows many are not yet in the labour market, and education levels remain a barrier: **42%** of those aged 10 and over had not reached the General Education Diploma level.

## What it means for job seekers

- **Private firms are hiring.** Nearly half of working Omanis with disabilities are in company jobs.
- **Ask about workplace adjustments.** Employers can adapt tasks, equipment and schedules.
- **Training improves chances.** Vocational courses linked to employers lead to steadier jobs.
- **Register with the Ministry of Labour.** Official job matching helps connect candidates with inclusive employers.
